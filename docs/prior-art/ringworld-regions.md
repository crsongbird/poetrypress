# Regions, borders and walls for the Painted Ringworld

Ruby asked (2026-10-25) for "towns with walls; eras side by side with clear borders; factions holding regions; names from
the seeds and the app's own esoteric language; crystals of a colour for each magical element ruling a region".
How to read the tags: [src](URL) is a page that was opened and read; [est] is an estimate, a computation or a design judgment.

## 1 · Town walls

- A lord's banner was "square-shaped, in contrast to the tapering standard or the pennon", bearing his family's arms;
  the Old French *baniere* from the lord's power of command, a "token of authority"
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_banneret). So in the painter [est]: every other tower of a whole wall flies
  its region's holder's square banner on a pole five metres over its top, its field above its charge, streaming
  downwind and rippling with the film's sway; none on a ruin.
- Behind the parapet, "a wall walk from which the defenders could fight or move"; after gunpowder "the height of the
  curtain walls was reduced" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtain_wall_(fortification)).
- The "crenel was typically one-third the width of the adjacent merlon" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlement);
  in France merlons near two metres tall, the sill a metre over the walk, crenels 0.7 to 1 m wide
  [src](https://techno-science.net/glossaire-definition/Creneau.html); Norwich had "40 merlons in a length of just over
  105 metres", merlons "about 2 metres wide", embrasures "about 300mm"
  [src](https://www.norwich.gov.uk/site/custom_scripts/citywalls/01/report.php): a tooth every 2.6 m [est].
- Towers "not more than a bowshot apart", so each can drive attackers off its neighbour, the walk broken at each by a
  loose floor to cut away [src](https://lexundria.com/vitr/1.5.4/mg); Conwy's 21 open-backed towers "about 46m" apart,
  crossed by "removable wooden bridges", the wall 9 m high and 1.68 m thick, towers to 15 m, "three double-towered
  gates" [src](https://castlewales.com/conwytw.html).
- Roman Saint-Bertrand's towers "just over 26m" apart, under 4 m across, a storey over the walk [src](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-3-3379-3388-wood.pdf);
  Norwich's 48 m apart down a 16 m drop, one 6.3 m across [src](https://www.norwich.gov.uk/site/custom_scripts/citywalls/33/report.php).
- Square towers are roomy but "the corners are vulnerable to" mining; round ones resist sappers and missiles; D-shaped
  ones turn the curve out; gates have towers "on either side" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortified_tower).
- Ávila: 2,516 m round 31 ha, 88 semicircular towers, nine gates, the wall 12 m high and 3 m thick, two gates "flanked
  by twin towers, 20 metres (66 ft) high, linked by a semicircular arch" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_%C3%81vila).
- Carcassonne 3 km of double walls, 52 towers [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cit%C3%A9_de_Carcassonne); York 3.4
  km, "generally 13 feet (4 m) high" on a rampart, 39 towers, four main bars [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_city_walls);
  Rothenburg "3,400 m", 42 of some 70 towers left [src](https://www.european-traveler.com/germany/walking-on-the-free-ramparts-and-town-walls-of-rothenburg-ob-der-tauber/).
- Dubrovnik about 1,940 m, up to 25 m high, 4 to 6 m thick to the land [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Dubrovnik);
  Visby 3.44 of 3.6 km standing, 5 to 6 m raised 3 to 4 m, 29 large towers and 22 "saddle towers" on the wall, 2 m
  above the sea in the west, about 40 m in the east [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby_City_Wall).
- Ávila's circuit is 1.27 times the round of a circle of its area, a tower every 28.6 m; Visby's every 71 m [est].
- Pembroke's line "was dictated by the topography", "the wall forming a revetment", later quarried for stone, breached,
  two of three gates gone [src](https://www.pembrokeandmonktonhistory.org.uk/documents/PembrokeTownWalls-Introduction.pdf);
  York lost posterns, towers and barbicans to streets and a railway [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_city_walls);
  Visby has its Fallen Wall and a 10 m length that fell in 2012 [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visby_City_Wall);
  Norwich's River Lane wall is parts "little more than 300mm high" [src](https://www.norwich.gov.uk/site/custom_scripts/citywalls/01/report.php);
  early walls were often earth and timber, many now "Fragmentary" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_town_walls_in_England_and_Wales).
- So in the painter [est]: a curtain 6 to 12 m high (4 m on an earth bank, 20 m for a rich port), 1.5 to 3 m thick;
  merlons 1.5 to 2 m wide, 2 m over the walk, the sill 1 m up, crenels a third of a merlon, a tooth every 2 to 2.6 m;
  towers every 25 to 50 m and at each corner, 4 to 7 m across, a storey (5 to 8 m) over the curtain, square in an old
  town, round or D-shaped in a newer; a gate two towers 1.6 times the wall over an arch; circuits 0.7 to 3.5 km, 1.25
  times the round of a circle of the town's area, the top stepping down a slope a length at a time; a ruin keeping 50
  to 90 per cent, gaps of 5 to 60 m where roads pass, rubble a third the wall's height, stumps under 0.5 m; under 20 px
  tall the merlons become a toothed line, under 6 px a plain band.

## 2 · Boundary stones and border signs

- German stones: main stones "up to about 1.50 m" above ground and over a metre deep, 250 to 600 m apart, the oldest
  tapering, later ones squared; plain runner stones between, lettered A to Z; three- and four-way stones where lands
  meet; on the faces each neighbour's arms or initials, the year, numbers running counterclockwise; on top a groove
  along the line, bent where it turns; witnesses (charcoal, shards) buried beneath
  [src](https://www.bodenseekreis.de/fileadmin/06_bildung_kultur/kultur/kreisarchiv/kleindenkmale/Historie_der_Grenzsteine.pdf).
- A parish stone at Cobham (1808): Portland stone, "9 inches square", "10 inches above ground", "S and P" on the east
  face, "C and P" on the west, the date on the north [src](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1346416);
  London's are wall plaques of parish letters and a date [src](https://exploring-london.com/tag/parishes/).
- Washington's 40 stones (1791 to 1792), a mile apart, "4 feet tall and 12 inches square", half buried, a cut pyramid on
  top [src](https://boundarystones.org/articles/americanspirit_2011.pdf); "Jurisdiction of the United States" toward the
  District, Virginia or Maryland behind [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_markers_of_the_original_District_of_Columbia).
- Canada and the US: Monument One (1843), a 14 ft (4.3 m) cast-iron obelisk [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_One);
  island monuments "6-FOOT HIGH", each commissioner's name toward his country
  [src](https://collection.ingenium.ca/item/1990.0321.001); 5,528 land monuments on a "6 metre or 20 foot wide clear
  vista" [src](https://internationalboundarycommission.org/en/the-boundary-and-you/interesting-facts.php).
- Missouri's STATE LINE, COUNTY LINE and CITY LIMIT signs stand at the line, "white legend on a green background",
  naming only the place [src](https://epg.modot.org/index.php/903.9_General_Information_Signs); Germany's "gelben
  Ortstafeln" mark a town's start and end [src](https://www.anwaltonline.com/verkehrsrecht/tipps/1677/geschlossene-ortschaft/),
  both faces back to back on a two-way road [src](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:traffic_sign%3DDE:310).
- So in the painter [est]: where a road crosses a border, a stone 0.3 m square, 0.6 to 1.5 m tall, each holder's initial
  in its banner colour on the face toward it, a year and a number on the sides; by it a board 1.2 by 0.6 m on a post
  naming the region entered, in its colours, the region left on its back; runner stones every 250 to 600 m off the
  roads, a 4 m obelisk where three regions meet, a 6 m strip cleared through forest; drawn only nearer than 300 m.

## 3 · Heraldry for banners

- The metals or (gold) and argent (silver); the colours gules (red), azure (blue), sable (black), vert (green), purpure
  (purple), tenné (orange), sanguine; furs ermine and vair [src](https://rarebooks.library.nd.edu/digital/heraldry/colors.html).
  "There are no fixed shades": or yellow or gold, argent white or grey [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tincture_(heraldry)).
- The rule: "metal should not be put on metal, nor colour on colour" (Llwyd, 1568), so arms are "easily recognisable";
  a divided field is exempt [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_tincture).
- Divisions: per pale, per fess, per bend, per saltire, quarterly, per chevron, per pall, tierced, on lines engrailed,
  indented, wavy or embattled [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_the_field); ordinaries "theoretically" a
  third, fess and pale 1/5 to 1/3 of the field, the cross the two joined [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_(heraldry)).
- A banner of arms is "square or oblong" with "the entire coat of arms"; the standard tapers (usually 120 to 60 cm),
  St George's cross by the staff, then two livery colours with badges; a pennon rides a lance; a gonfalon hangs
  vertically, as in Florence [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldic_flag).
- Courtois (1414) paired or with topaz and the Sun, argent pearl and Moon, gules ruby and Mars, azure sapphire and
  Jupiter, vert emerald and Venus, purpure amethyst and Mercury, sable diamond and Saturn; Bartolo (1350s) gave azure to
  air and gules to fire [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tincture_(heraldry)).
- So in the painter [est]: each faction one colour and one metal, its device one of eight that read at a few pixels (a
  chief, per pale, per fess, per bend, quarterly, per saltire, a cross, a bend), bands a third of the field, no two
  neighbours sharing both tinctures; banners 2:3 tall, 1.5 by 2.2 m, from crossbars on towers and gates; an element's
  region takes its gem: fire gules (ruby), air azure (sapphire), water argent (pearl), earth vert (emerald), crystals of
  that colour standing where its border stones would be, its banners that colour on or.

## 4 · Political borders in map generators

- Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator works on Voronoi cells from a Delaunay triangulation
  [src](https://git.korz.dev/backups/Fantasy-Map-Generator/src/commit/05c53d276aaa1153f8d61c6ab515f79fccd6744f/wiki/Modules-Reference.md);
  "Each capital becomes center of a state", "Boundaries form where states meet", "Neutral areas remain unclaimed"
  [src](https://git.korz.dev/backups/Fantasy-Map-Generator/raw/commit/05c53d276aaa1153f8d61c6ab515f79fccd6744f/wiki/Generation-Process.md);
  states grow by "a priority-queue flood-fill across the Voronoi cell graph", cheaper through their own culture (a
  generated digest of the code) [src](https://deepwiki.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/3.3-states-and-provinces).
- Costs by culture type: water "6 times the size of the cell", river cells 20 to 100 more; naval peoples 2 times over
  water, 100 more inland; nomads 50 times over water, forests 10 times; expansionism 0.7 (hunters) to 1.5 (naval,
  nomads) [src](https://git.korz.dev/backups/Fantasy-Map-Generator/wiki/Culture-types); each given "a budget (multiplied
  by the Growth rate setting)" [src](https://git.korz.dev/backups/Fantasy-Map-Generator/wiki/commit/fd10ac5492fad0ed0cf91545a969ae10c7a9eb92.diff);
  a low growth rate leaves land "politically neutral" [src](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/wiki/Quick-Start-Tutorial).
- Civilization V's borders grow "one or more tiles at a time", "favoring more productive tiles" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_V);
  Dwarf Fortress runs history 5 to 1,050 years, the longer leaving more sacked towns [src](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:World_gen);
  Red Blob's strategy maps give each Voronoi cell one terrain [src](https://redblobgames.com/x/1638-voronoi-strategy-mapgen).
- So in the painter [est]: a region per capital on the land's cells, grown by least cost: 1 a cell, 2 more per 100 m
  climbed, 30 for a river, 60 for a ridge crest, 6 times over water, so rivers and ridges become borders by cost alone;
  a budget per faction (its might times the seed's growth), wilds left between; 3 to 6 provinces seeded at towns;
  borders on cell edges, smoothed, sharp where two meet; an elemental region cheap over its own ground (fire on desert,
  water on coast, air on peaks, earth on forest); eras side by side as regions of different ages, the older more ruined.

## 5 · Place names

- Old English unless marked: -ton "enclosure, estate, homestead"; -bury "fortified enclosure"; -ford a crossing; -wick
  "place, settlement"; -ham "farm, homestead"; -ley "a woodland clearing"; -chester "camp, fortification (of Roman
  origin)"; -stow "(holy) place (of assembly)"; -mere "lake, pool"; -dale "valley"; Old Norse -by "settlement, village",
  -thorpe "secondary settlement", -thwaite "a forest clearing with a dwelling"; weald or wold "high woodland"
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_generic_forms_in_place_names_in_the_British_Isles); ford "a
  shallow river crossing", wick "farm" [src](https://anglosaxondiscovery.ashmus.ox.ac.uk/Life/settlement/placenames.html);
  fell from Old Norse "'mountain' (usually a large, flat mountain)", with beck, dale, howe, pike and tarn
  [src](https://www.walklakes.co.uk/toponymy.html).
- A march is a borderland, against a heartland, sometimes a buffer two realms held; Old Norse mǫrk "borderland,
  forest", as in Denmark; its lords became marquesses and margraves [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_(territory));
  weald is Old English for "forest", cognate of German Wald [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weald).
- Azgaar's names walk a Markov chain learnt from real names per culture (German 5 to 12 letters), a state named from its
  capital by stripping -berg or -ton and adding -ia, -land or -terra (a generated digest)
  [src](https://deepwiki.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/3.1-names-and-language-generation); order-n chains with Katz
  back-off need about a hundred training words [src](https://github.com/Tw1ddle/markov-namegen-lib); Totro alternates
  consonant and vowel units, 2 to 10 [src](https://dwheeler.com/totro.html); O'Leary's naming languages use a small
  sound set, syllables S?CVF, "One syllable equals one morpheme", a 'city' morpheme reused, a particle "perhaps meaning
  'the'", one spelling system, names of 5 to 12 letters [src](https://www.mewo2.com/notes/naming-language/).
- So in the painter [est]: a name is a stem of 1 to 3 syllables from the seed's sound set plus an element word of the
  app's language with one fixed meaning (fort, crossing, clearing, farm, hill, march, forest, crystal), spelled one way
  and reused, so every ford town ends alike; walled towns take the fort word, open ones the farm word; a border region
  "the March of" its stem; a region named from its capital's stem; 5 to 12 letters, painted on each border's board.

# Star-Cluster Release Names: the series after Neptune's moons

**Why this file exists.** When Neptune's named moons run out, releases take the form **"<Star cluster>–<Member
star>"**. Clusters run nearest to Earth first. Inside each cluster, its named members run nearest first, the way the moon
series ran outward from each planet. This file is that list, researched 2026-10-21, with the sources it rests on.

**Key:** [src](…) is a fact stated in the linked source, read this session. [est] is my own decision or arithmetic: a
parsec value converted at 1 pc = 3.2616 ly, a tie-break, or a suggested title form.

**Rules applied**
- **Named stars only.** "IAU" means the name is in the IAU Catalog of Star Names (IAU-CSN, copy last updated 2022-04-04)
  [src](https://exopla.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/IAU-CSN_2021_pas.rochester.edu_emamajek_WGSN_IAU-CSN.txt.pdf) or
  in a later WGSN batch (2024–2025 [src](https://iau.org/IAU/News/Ann2026/New-Star-Names-2026.aspx); 2026 names from
  the star's own article). Every star in the main list has an IAU name. Traditional-only names appear in the Notes.
- **Members only.** Membership comes from the cluster article, the star's own article, or a membership study. If those
  sources disagree, or only say "may be", the row is marked **Disputed** with the reason.
- **Member distance.** Each star's own measured distance, taken from its Wikipedia article. The Pleiades are the
  exception: they use the cluster article's member table (see Notes, section 1). The basis (Hipparcos or Gaia) is given
  where the source states it.
- **Cluster distance.** From the cluster article or the cited paper. For Sco–Cen, the classical de Zeeuw et al. (1999)
  subgroup distances are used throughout, so that the three subgroups share one basis.

## Notes: choices and disputes

**1. How distances were chosen, and where the order is fragile**
- *Pleiades.* The bright Pleiads' individual parallaxes are poor. Merope is "too bright" for Gaia
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merope_%28star%29), and Hipparcos's Pleiades parallaxes carry a known error
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleiades). Several star articles just repeat the cluster's 444 ly. The cluster
  article's table is the one source that gives every Pleiad a value of its own, so it sets the order. Its spread
  (344–434 ly) is mostly measurement error around a single 444 ly cluster, so this order is a convention rather than real
  depth [est].
- *Hyades.* Ain is clearly nearest of the classic four, with a precise Gaia parallax. Prima Hyadum, Secunda Hyadum and
  Chamukuy (154–157 ly) overlap within their errors. The Taurus star list gives other values: θ² 149, δ¹ 153, γ 154,
  ε 155 ly [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stars_in_Taurus). Lembu's place depends on whether its Gaia DR3
  125 ly or the list's 189 ly is right (see its row) [est].
- *Ursa Major.* Alioth, Mizar and Phecda (82.6–83.2 ly) also overlap within errors [est].
- *Cluster order.* Two neighbours nearly tie. With de Zeeuw's Sco–Cen distances, the order is LCC 118 pc, Pleiades
  136 pc, UCL 140 pc, US 145 pc. With the Gaia-era values, UCL is 136 ± 5 pc, which ties the Pleiades
  (135.74–136.2 pc) [src](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.09483) [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleiades).
  The classical order is kept here [est].
- *Shell access.* SIMBAD and the WGSN's own host were unreachable from this session's network. Names were therefore checked
  against an archived copy of the IAU-CSN plus the 2024–2025 IAU announcement. Distances come from Wikipedia and the cited
  papers, not straight from SIMBAD [est].

**2. Title forms** [est]
- The en dash "–" belongs in the human-readable title. In git tags, use hyphens and strip spaces and diacritics, matching
  the earlier `PoetryPress-Vellum-Jupiter-Thebe` style. For example: `…-Alpha-Persei-Aldu`, `…-Hyades-Prima-Hyadum`.
- Three titles are subgroup short forms, not official names: "Crux" (LCC), "Lupus" (UCL) and "Scorpius" (US). The full
  names contain their own dashes. "Perseus" here means Perseus OB2, a different group from the Alpha Persei Cluster.
- "Hyades–Prima Hyadum" and "Hyades–Secunda Hyadum" repeat the cluster's own name. "Alpha Persei–Mirfak" is a tautology.
  All three are kept because they are the stars' proper names.

**3. Names that are not IAU, left out of the main order**
- **Coma Star Cluster (Melotte 111), 280 ly (86 pc)** [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma_Star_Cluster): it has no
  IAU-named member. 21 Com, called **Kissīn**, is "a confirmed physical member", at ~270 ly (Gaia DR3)
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_Comae_Berenices). That name is an obscure traditional one (Hyde, via Allen),
  so it fails "widely used" [est]. *Optional:* if Ruby accepts it, "Coma–Kissin" would slot between Hyades and Crux [est].
- **Girtab** (κ Sco), traditional; it is not in the IAU-CSN
  [src](https://exopla.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/IAU-CSN_2021_pas.rochester.edu_emamajek_WGSN_IAU-CSN.txt.pdf). It
  is a 2023 SigMA Sco–Cen member [src](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2023/09/aa43690-22/T5.html), at
  480 ± 10 ly [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappa_Scorpii). It sits south of the Galactic plane, outside the
  classical US/UCL/LCC areas, so it has no subgroup title [est].
- **Sterope II** (22 Tau), traditional. The IAU gave the shared "Sterope/Asterope" name to 21 Tau alone
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_Tauri).
- **Al Dafirah** (γ Com), traditional, and "probably not actually a member" of the Coma cluster
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_Comae_Berenices).

**4. IAU-named stars that are not members, or not confirmed, and so are left out**
- **Ursa Major:** Dubhe and Alkaid are not members [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursa_Major_moving_group). Sirius
  "may not be" a member; it seems too young [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursa_Major_moving_group). The named
  *stream* members are spread across the sky and are not the Collinder 285 core
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursa_Major_moving_group): Menkalinan, Alphecca, Skat, Adhafera, Zhou. They could be
  a separate "Ursa Major stream" series if ever wanted [est].
- **Hyades:** Aldebaran is foreground [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyades_%28star_cluster%29).
  **Shakata** (75 Tau, IAU 2026) was chosen because it sits in the middle of the V asterism
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75_Tauri), but it lies at 187 ly and no membership claim was found [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75_Tauri).
  **Gaja** (τ Tau, IAU 2026) belongs to the Taurion OB association, not the Hyades
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tau_Tauri).
- **Coma:** **Phyllon Kissinou** (23 Com, IAU 2024) is at 310 ly, and its article makes no membership claim
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23_Comae_Berenices). It is not in the cluster's brightest-members table
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma_Star_Cluster).
- **Sco–Cen:** Gacrux, θ Cen (Menkent) and ι Cen (**Kulou**, IAU 2025) are foreground
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpius%E2%80%93Centaurus_association). ζ Oph is a runaway [src](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0809.0407),
  and its traditional name "Han" is not in the IAU-CSN
  [src](https://exopla.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/IAU-CSN_2021_pas.rochester.edu_emamajek_WGSN_IAU-CSN.txt.pdf). **Aiolos** (HD 95086) "was originally thought to be part of" LCC
  but "may be instead part of the Carina association" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD_95086). Three more are IAU
  names, but no membership claim was found in any source read: **Shaula** (λ Sco, ~570 ly
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_Scorpii); an orbit gives 112 ± 5 pc
  [src](https://iac.es/en/science-and-technology/publications/orbital-elements-masses-and-distance-l-scorpii-and-b-determined-sydney-university)),
  **Lesath** (υ Sco, 580 ly) [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upsilon_Scorpii) and **Leepwal** (ζ Cen, 382 ly)
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_Centauri). Any of these can be added if a membership source turns up.
- **IC 2602** (486 ly) and **IC 2391**: no member has a proper name
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IC_2602) [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IC_2391). Skipped.

**5. Single-name groups (optional extensions, not in the order)**
The brief asked for extra groups only when they have several named members. These have just one, so they are listed here
with where they would slot in [est]:
- **32 Orionis group: Bellatrix** (γ Ori, IAU), 250 ± 10 ly. Its membership is in question: Bouy & Alves 2015 called it
  "probable"; Bell et al. 2017 doubted it because of its proper motion [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32_Orionis)
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellatrix). It would slot after the Hyades.
- **Lambda Orionis Cluster (Collinder 69), 1,300 ly (400 pc): Meissa** (λ Ori, IAU), ~1,300 ly, "the dominant member"
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_Orionis_Cluster) [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meissa). It would slot
  after Orion.
- **NGC 1980 (Orion OB1c): Hatysa** (ι Ori, IAU), ~1,340 ly by Hipparcos. It is "generally assumed to be associated
  with" NGC 1980 [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iota_Orionis). It would slot after Lambda Orionis.

**6. Name coverage caveat**
Names are still being adopted: Lembu, Shakata and Gaja came in 2026. Through this session's fetch tool, the Wikipedia name
list rendered only from A to "Shaula" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_proper_names_of_stars). The April 2026
release post lists 26 names without designations, and none looked like a cluster star
[src](https://exopla.net/releases-in-2026/). The exopla.net post for May 2026 is listed as protected
(https://exopla.net/2026/04/, seen in search results only) [est]. A 2026 name starting Sh–Z on a cluster member could
therefore be missing. Recheck before the series starts [est].

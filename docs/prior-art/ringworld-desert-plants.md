## 1 · The saguaro

- Growth is slow and depends on rain: some "1 to 3 inches tall" at 5 to 10 years, 2 feet at 20 to 40, 6 to 8 feet at
  35 to 70 years [src](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/learn/nature/saguaro-growth.htm).
- In the drier Organ Pipe country, about 6.5 feet at 70 years and 15 to 16 feet at 95 to 100, when "it may start its
  first arm"; average height about 40 feet, the tallest recorded over 78 feet; a foot of saguaro "can weigh up to 90
  pounds" [src](https://www.nps.gov/orpi/learn/nature/saguaro-cactus.htm).
- Arms "begin at about 8 to 20 feet tall, but some saguaros never grow any"; the tallest recorded, at 78 feet, had none
  [src](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/learn/nature/saguaro-growth.htm).
- The first arms come "around 50 to 70 years old", in some places 100; branching seems to depend "on size rather than
  age", some arming at "around 3.25 meters"; the first arm tends to face "the south or southeast", the second "often
  grows facing north", later ones keeping the plant balanced; "mature saguaro often have many arms"
  [src](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/learn/saguaro-arms-project.htm).
- Some have "dozens of arms", others "never produce a single one" [src](https://www.nps.gov/orpi/learn/nature/saguaro-cactus.htm).
- Flowers from late April to mid June, each open 18 to 24 hours [src](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/learn/nature/saguaro-growth.htm);
  peak in "the last week of May through the first week in June", on the top of each stem and arm (the "crown"), "large
  white flowers with yellow centers"; the fruit "ripens into a deep red color in late June and early July"
  [src](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/getinvolved/saguaro-flower-power-project.htm).
- Seedlings grow in the shade of a nurse plant (a palo verde in the park's photo), which the grown cactus later
  outcompetes for water [src](https://www.nps.gov/orpi/learn/nature/saguaro-cactus.htm).
- So in the painter [est]: a saguaro's width stays some 0.6 m whatever its height; no arms under about 3.3 m; then about
  one more arm for every 1.7 m of height, give or take two, and now and then one that never arms; each arm at its own
  bearing round the column (the first southerly, the second northerly), drawn behind the column and in its shade when
  its bearing is away from the eye, before it and lit when toward; old ones' arms sometimes drooping, now and then an arm
  on an arm; flowers on every crown from late April to mid June (0.31 to 0.45 of the year), red fruit to mid July; a
  young one usually beside its nurse tree.

## 2 · The others

- Organ pipe: "30 foot high stems" in clumps of "many dozens", each stem 15 to 20 ribbed and about 6 inches across;
  white or pale pink night flowers near the stem tops; round spiny greenish-red fruit
  [src](https://www.americansouthwest.net/plants/cacti/stenocereus-thurberi.html). [est]: drawn to 7 m here, many stems
  from one foot, bloom in May and June.
- Fishhook barrel: about 0.7 m wide and 0.9 to 1.8 m tall (to 3 m), ball-shaped growing cylindrical, "15-28 ribs",
  leaning south ("compass barrel cactus"), yellow to red-orange flowers atop it through the summer, yellow fruit kept
  "sometimes for more than a year" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferocactus_wislizeni).
- Prickly pear: pads "flattened and oval to elongated", "generally between 4 and 18 inches long"; flowers in orange,
  yellow, red or pink in spring and summer; "bright red, egg-shaped fruits"
  [src](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/plants/ornamentals/prickly-pear/); in Tucson they bloom in May and June,
  the fruit harvested in July and August, Opuntia engelmannii's yellow-flowered and purple-fruited
  [src](https://thisistucson.com/tucsonlife/article_4913a914-6d1a-11e9-b393-534714927bc6.html).
- Teddy-bear cholla: "1 to 5 ft" with "a distinct trunk", nearly horizontal branches at its top, the lower ones falling
  away; silvery-white spines almost hiding the stems; yellow-green flowers in May and June; the trunk darkening with age
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindropuntia_bigelovii). [est]: its spines drawn brighter with the sun behind.
- Ocotillo: "up to 20 feet", "long cane-like unbranched spiny stems" from a short trunk, "small 2 inch leaves" when there
  is moisture, red tubular flower clusters at the tips from March through June
  [src](https://www.desertmuseum.org/kids/oz/long-fact-sheets/Ocotillo.php). [est]: leafed in the spring and summer
  rains, bare grey between.
- Agave [est]: a rosette of thick, pointed grey-green leaves; once, when old, one tall branched flower stalk.

# The magic world's creatures for the Painted Ringworld

Ruby, 2026-10-25, under magic by element: "cute slimes, chocobo-like birds and such (legally distinct)". How to read the
tags: [src](URL) is a page that was opened and read; [est] is an estimate, a computation or a design judgment.

## 1 · Slimes

- Slimes came to games from horror: Lovecraft's shoggoths, "shapeless beings made of black slime"; Dungeons & Dragons
  (1974) took its oozes partly from the film The Blob, the gelatinous cube enveloping its prey; Wizardry made slimes
  low-level monsters and so inspired Dragon Quest's; after it games often made them cute, as enemies, allies or pets
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slime_(monster)).
- Dragon Quest's slime was asked for as "a pile of goo"; Akira Toriyama drew it as a "tear-drop", with a "weird smile";
  its kin come in other colours and shapes (a pool, a shell, tentacles, a gem, a cube, a crown, metal)
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slime_(Dragon_Quest)).
- Squash and stretch: "an object's volume does not change when squashed or stretched"; a ball squashes at impact and
  stretches as it falls and after it bounces, its width contracting as it stretches; anticipation prepares an action
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation).
- So in the painter [est]: to be its own and not Dragon Quest's, a slime here is a low mound of jelly wider than tall
  (no teardrop's point, no smile): see-through, its darker heart showing inside it, a gleam on the light's side, two
  small dark eyes. It hops every second or two of its own, sitting or on its way: flat as it gathers itself, stretched
  in the air, squashed as it lands, a jelly's wobble after, its volume kept. Its colour is its region's element; by
  night it glows faintly. Half a metre across, one to four together, about the meadows and woods' edges of the magic
  world.

## 2 · Striders, the tall runners

- The ostrich runs a steady 60 km/h and over 70 at its top, with a step of about 5 m, the longest of the running birds;
  its legs are the longest for its size, the muscle high on the thigh and the lower leg light and worked by long
  tendons; it walks on its toes (two) [src](https://scienceinschool.org/?p=97574).
- The emu runs "grounded", one foot always touching the ground, more like racewalking than a sprint; it cannot
  straighten its legs fully [src](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/why-does-the-emus-run-look-so-strange).
- The emu stands up to 1.9 m, 18 to 60 kg, in "grey-brown plumage of shaggy appearance", furry near the skin and like
  grass at the ends; its neck pale blue, showing through sparse feathers; wings vestigial, about 20 cm; legs bare and
  among the strongest of any animal; 48 km/h, strides of about 1 m walking and up to 2.75 m at a gallop; mostly in
  pairs, at times in large flocks, foraging by day [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu).
- The chocobo: a galliform bird usually of yellow feathers, its "Kweh" call, made by Koichi Ishii from memories of a
  chick he raised, its name from a chocolate confection; ridden across the world map, raced
  [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocobo).
- So in the painter [est]: to be its own, a strider is built as an emu is, not as a chocobo: some two metres tall, a
  deep body under shaggy plumes hanging like grass, a long bare bluish neck, a small head with a short beak and a fan
  of plumes on its crown, little wings, a plumed tail, long bare legs that never straighten, two toes; its plumage of
  its region's element, never one brand's yellow. Two to five graze the open country of the magic world, walking at
  1.4 m/s, bolting at 12 with their necks stretched forward, a stride of 2.75 m, now and then sitting to rest.

## 3 · Drakes

- A wyvern has "two legs, two wings, and often a pointed tail" and is "rarely fire-breathing"; in British heraldry two
  legs to a dragon's four [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyvern).
- A game's dragon flight: ballistic, with impulses from its flaps, a glide pose held some 30 degrees from level
  [src](https://frozenfractal.com/blog/2016/4/15/flight-of-the-dragon/).
- So in the painter [est]: a drake some four metres across the wings, soaring on the rising air as a raptor does
  (`wkDrake`), so that its motion is a bird of prey's and its shape is not: its arm out to the wrist and three long
  fingers fanned behind it, the membrane between them scalloped along the trailing edge and a little see-through, a
  long neck and horned head ahead, a long tail ending in a spade. Its hide of its region's element. One or two over the
  magic world by day, grounded in the rain.

## 4 · Sky rays

- The sky or space whale is an old trope of games: the arcwhales of Skies of Arcadia, Beyond Good & Evil, Final Fantasy
  IV, the Dishonored series and others [src](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Space_Whale).
- A manta ray's stroke averages 0.31 Hz (about 3.2 s a beat), its fin tips sweeping 0.74 of its length peak to peak on
  a sinusoidal path, its body hardly pitching [src](https://sicb.org/?p=26544). Baleen whales cruise at "roughly
  invariant … 2 m s−1", their tails beating slower as they grow longer
  [src](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article/222/20/jeb204172/224449/Scaling-of-swimming-performance-in-baleen-whales).
- So in the painter [est]: rays nearly forty metres across, rowing round a wide circle of their own 170 to 380 m up at
  4.5 m/s, one to three in a pod, one behind another, rising and sinking slowly on the air (`cruise`, `wkRay`). A beat
  every seven seconds (slower than a manta's, as something so big would be), a wave running out along the wings; seen
  from below and to the side, pale beneath, the horns of the head fins forward, a long whip of a tail; by night a row
  of steady lights of the region's colour along each wing. On any ring with magic in it they drift anywhere over it.

## 5 · Lantern moths

- The "blue ghost" firefly (Phausis reticulata) gives a steady pale glow for 30–40 s, not a flash; displays begin at
  dusk [src](https://home.nps.gov/grsm/learn/nature/firefly-flash-patterns.htm). Nothing may flash "more than three
  times in any one-second period" (WCAG 2.3.1)
  [src](https://www.getstark.co/wcag-explained/operable/seizures-and-physical-reactions/three-flashes-or-below-threshold).
- So in the painter [est]: pale soft-winged moths hawking slowly a metre or so up about the woods and waters of the
  magic world and the ruins, only by night, each with a soft glow round it of its region's light, breathing on its own
  three-and-a-half-second clock (`wkMoth`), never a flash.

## 6 · Moonharts

- The white stag of Celtic and Arthurian lore is an "otherworldly messenger", a sign of "sacred or forbidden grounds",
  uncatchable in the quests [src](https://islandfolklore.com/the-white-stag/). The qilin has a deer's body and "one
  horn", "always shown with cloven hooves", its mane flowing upward [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qilin); the
  unicorn a "single large, pointed, spiraling horn" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicorn).
- So in the painter [est]: pale deer, two to four, where the magic world's woods meet its meadows, grazing and bolting
  as deer do; one long spiral horn from the brow, ivory, and a mane of the region's light along the neck, glowing
  softly by night (`wkQuad`). Of folklore, a deer and not a horse, and of no one's game.

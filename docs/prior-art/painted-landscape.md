# Prior art: land and water textures in a painted landscape

Research for Painted Landscape's brushwork, after the artist asked for "more variety in the water and land textures". Today each country works every band with one stroke kind (grass, leaf, rock, dot, snow, field or long), and water has three moods (still, ruffled, choppy) of short level strokes, knife-cut lines of light and a sun path. This note gathers how painters, geologists, sailors and game artists make each kind of ground and water recognizable from a distance. The river's own course, value and reflections are in painted-rivers.md.

**Tags.** [src](URL) marks a claim from a source opened and read for this note. [est] marks an estimate, a computation or a design judgement. d is distance along the ground; D is depth below the horizon in focal lengths (the tangent of the angle down); f ≈ 3300 px is the focal length of a 3072 px frame about 50° across; the eye is H = 1.6 m above flat ground unless marked.

## Summary

- **Value comes first.** Planes take their values from their angle to the sky: sky lightest, flat ground next, slopes darker, upright things (trees) darkest, and snow can make the ground lighter than the sky [src](https://theartistsroad.net/articles/thecarlsontheoryofangles). Kuindzhi often led the eye from foreground to background through alternating bands of shadow and light [src](https://www.tretyakovgallerymagazine.com/node/6377): the band structure this surface already has.
- **Each ground has its own mark.** In one painting Van Gogh used diagonal strokes for standing wheat, shorter random marks for its heads and swirling loops for weeds [src](https://eclecticlight.co/2019/10/21/work-in-progress-vincent-van-goghs-wheat-field-with-cypresses/), with the stalks in rhythmic hatching and the ears in clotted impasto [src](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/works-of-art/1993.132). A knife gives short upward strokes for grass, edge-cut cracks for rock and flat level strokes for calm water [src](https://en.gallerix.ru/tech/ispolzovanie-mastixina-v-peyzazhnoy-zhivopisi).
- **What stands up shrinks; what lies down flattens.** A blade or a tree keeps its shape and shrinks as 1/d. A furrow, a ripple or a flower drift lying on the ground is also squashed to D of its width: 1:11 at 5° below the horizon and 1:2 at 26°. Rows running toward the eye stay visible about 40 times farther than rows running across it [est, computed §15].
- **Haze sets how far detail lasts.** Apparent contrast decays exponentially with distance [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visibility). At a quarter of the visual range, a texture keeps about 38% of its contrast [est, computed]. Painters keep large, thick, textured marks in front, and thin, soft, simplified ones behind [src](https://www.virtualartacademy.com/?p=2210) [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/atmospheric-perspective/).
- **Masses, not leaves.** Paint a forest as "a few larger, simpler masses" in about four values, with a few sky holes [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/blogs/plein-air/see-the-forest-not-the-trees). Distant trees become "a flat, patterned shape" of one value [src](https://www.outdoorpainter.com/?p=18056).
- **Water's moods have a scale.** The Beaufort scale describes what the moods should paint: 0 "Sea like a mirror", 1 "Ripples with the appearance of scales", 2 crests with "a glassy appearance", 3 "scattered white horses", 4 "fairly frequent", 5 "many" [src](https://www.weather.gov/ajk/beaufort).
- **Wind writes on water in patches.** Gusts are dark ovals that spread and then travel [src](https://www.sailingworld.com/how-to/how-to-call-puffs-upwind-and-down/). A steady light wind draws white foam lines along itself [src](https://blog.limnology.wisc.edu/2021/10/14/langmuir-circulation-explaining-lines-of-bubbles-on-the-surface-of-a-lake/). Water under a sheltering shore stays still [src](https://www.naturalnavigator.com/news/2022/02/what-is-a-wind-shadow/).

## 1. Grass and meadow (grassland, plains, river valley)

- **From afar.** Foreground grass shows full blades, shadows and stalks; distant grass shows mainly the tops of the plants, with light falling over them: "No detail in the back". Strokes follow the way the grass grows. Nearer grass is "more chromatic and lower in value", farther grass less chromatic and higher [src](https://www.wetcanvas.com/forums/topic/colors-in-the-foreground-middle-ground-and-background-for-grass). Near green is warmer and yellower, far green cooler and bluer [src](https://www.martakonieczny.art.pl/text-en-how-to-paint-a-meadow-not-a-green-blur_2_10_66.html).
- **As layers.** A meadow is a dense dark mass, then single stems and seed heads, then gaps of sky or distance: "painted as a colour when it should be painted as layers". A viewer knows a meadow from "a dozen or so forms": paint two or three foreground plants fully and suggest the rest [src](https://www.martakonieczny.art.pl/text-en-how-to-paint-a-meadow-not-a-green-blur_2_10_66.html). Bob Ross taps a 2-inch brush held at 45°, in layers [src](https://www.quarto.com/public/dynamic/files/uploadfile/2022/11/20221104153218395_Painting_with_Bob_Ross___Around_the_Bend.pdf). Distant clumps get rounded crowns rather than flat tops [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/9534).
- **Value.** Fields are Carlson's "half-light" plane, and beginners paint them too dark [src](https://theartistsroad.net/articles/thecarlsontheoryofangles).
- **Wind.** Honami are waves that travel over uniform canopies of crops and grass: a sweep of air bends a group of stalks, and the waves move faster than the mean wind [src](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dfd/2023/zc43/1) [src](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/abs/modelling-waving-crops-using-largeeddy-simulation-comparison-with-experiments-and-a-linear-stability-analysis/5362233D71E2866819F611F9C14607CE). Bent stalks show a different face to the sun, so a honami reads as a travelling patch, lighter or darker than the field [est].
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: lean and wind, blade length, clump scale, dry or green (a value shift), a band of seed heads, one to three honami patches.

## 2. Flowers in a meadow

- **From afar.** Paint distant flowers as a mass of colour, in patches that follow the contours of the landscape, with sky colour added to the farthest. Add a few odd patches of a different colour [src](https://onlineartlessons.com/?p=613). Near flowers are larger and shrink as they recede [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/9448).
- **How.** Lay the dark ground first so flowers don't float, treat the field as one mass, then put a few detailed blooms inside it and along its edges [src](https://www.outdoorpainter.com/?p=33402). In Monet's Poppy Field, "A few strokes of green plus the red poppies create the illusion of the tall, sweeping grass". A weak red, close in value and saturation to the weak green, does most of the work, and a strong red is kept for accents [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/the-poppy-field/). Thick paint lets seed heads catch the light [src](https://www.martakonieczny.art.pl/text-en-how-to-paint-a-meadow-not-a-green-blur_2_10_66.html).
- **In a grey painting** [est]: flowers are only values, light (daisies, cow parsley) or dark (poppies against pale grass). Most sit within a step of the grass, and a few accents stand out.
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: species (light or dark), drift size and count, which bands carry them, a single accent colour under Color.

## 3. Fields: crops, furrows and rows (fields, plains)

- **How.** Furrows go "broader and darker towards the foreground". The field darkens and warms toward the viewer, and cools as it recedes. Soil marks are dragged with the brush almost flat, and are lighter in the distance [src](https://onlineartlessons.com/?p=4844). In Meier's haystacks, the ploughed ground is planar, but overlapping strokes suggest its volume [src](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall00/cs597b/papers/meier96.pdf).
- **Following the land.** Contour ploughing runs rows across the slope along the hill's own lines, usually with strip cropping, which alternates crops in narrow bands [src](https://www.phmc.state.pa.us/portal/communities/agriculture/field-guide/contour-plowing.html).
- **Painters' fields.** Shishkin's Rye keeps pines standing in the field, a track leading in, and two heads "barely visible amid the rye" for scale [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye_%28Shishkin%29). Levitan's Vladimirka puts a yellow stripe of ripening rye near the horizon, in broad grey-blue strokes [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimirka_%28painting%29).
- **Geometry** [est, computed]. Take rows 0.75 m apart. Rows running across the view are f·s·H/d² apart on screen, so they close to 1 px by about 60 m. Rows running toward the eye are f·s/d apart, so they stay 1 px apart out to about 2.5 km. A far field therefore reads as rows converging on a point of the horizon, or as strips of crop, never as rows across it.
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: row direction (toward the eye, diagonal, along the contour), strip widths and their two values, stubble, plough or standing crop, hedgerow lines between fields.

## 4. Forest canopy from afar (forest, jungle, river valley)

- **Masses.** Squint and paint only the masses. Aim for four value changes or fewer, and look for the "tree-holes" [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/blogs/plein-air/see-the-forest-not-the-trees). Distant trees need a varied edge with a few hard spots and "a single value with little to no detail" [src](https://www.outdoorpainter.com/?p=18056). Distant trees get a narrower tonal range and are paler and bluer; trees are usually darker than grass [src](https://en.gallerix.ru/tech/zhivopis-derevev-i-lesov-rekomendacii-dlya-xudozhnikov).
- **Edges and placement.** Use rounded lines for deciduous canopy and sharp vertical spikes for conifers. Forest follows ridges, detailed along the edges so the hill still shows, with a faint dark shadow at its base for bulk [src](https://torstan.deviantart.com/art/How-to-Draw-Forests-295836612). Bob Ross's far treetops are tiny and soft, blended into the sky; his middle evergreens are distinct [src](https://www.quarto.com/public/dynamic/files/uploadfile/2022/11/20221104153218395_Painting_with_Bob_Ross___Around_the_Bend.pdf).
- **Levitan.** In Trail in Deciduous Forest, the background is a flat wash of green, and detail emerges toward the front like something out of a dense fog. Dabs of broken colour build the bark, ground and leaves; there are no true highlights, and specks of bare canvas flicker [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/trail-in-deciduous-forest/).
- **Jungle.** Emergent trees are "considerably taller than all the rest", with spreading crowns [src](https://striresearch.si.edu/rainforest/home/rainforest-layers/). Draw a background silhouette, sparse palms in the middle ground, and leaf masses darkening toward the bottom, with mist denser in the distance and in the middle ground too [src](https://dessindigo.com/en/blog/drawing-tutorial/drawing-jungle).
- **Scale** [est, computed]. A 10 m crown is 7 px wide at 5 km and under 2 px at 20 km. Mid bands can show crowns as bumps along the ridge; far bands only roughen the edge.
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: crown shape (round, spiky, umbrella), clump size, edge roughness, the number of sky holes, emergents, mist between bands.

## 5. Rock faces, cliffs, scree and strata (mountains, mesas, lake)

- **Planes.** Simplify rock into boxes. Each change of plane should bring a change of colour. Highlights on rock are rare and small, and dark accents go in cracks and at the base. For many small rocks, lay a base and suggest a few on top [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/paint-rocks-cliffs/). Rocks are rarely lighter than the sky; glacial deposits and mineral veins often run in a consistent direction [src](https://streamlinepublishing.com/inside-art/rocks-are-hard-three-quick-tips-for-painting-better-ones/). Paint cracks darker with a lighter lip on one side, and lighten the edges that stick out [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/9534).
- **Knife.** Use uneven strokes in varied directions, cutting cracks with the knife's edge. Sgraffito scratches back to the layer beneath [src](https://en.gallerix.ru/tech/ispolzovanie-mastixina-v-peyzazhnoy-zhivopisi).
- **Scree** collects at the foot of a cliff. It is concave upward, gentler at the foot, and sorted by size, with the larger pieces at the bottom [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scree). Dry sand rests at about 34°, natural gravel at 25–30° and granite at 35–40° [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_of_repose).
- **Mesas** have horizontal strata in "cliff-and-bench topography": resistant layers form cliffs and weaker ones gentler benches, under a resistant caprock [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesa). One watercolourist folds plastic wrap along the strata so the texture follows them [src](https://americanwatercolor.net/?p=2277).
- **Mountains by distance.** Far ranges can show no light-and-shadow contrast at all, while near ones get strong contrast; Payne paints them mostly purple, with lighter grey snow [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/how-to-paint-mountains-with-depth/).
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: strata spacing and dip, the cliff-to-bench ratio, fracture direction, scree aprons, boulder count.

## 6. Volcano

- **Shape.** A composite cone's profile is "concave-upward", with steep upper flanks grading into low aprons. Gullying is the commonest sign of age: gullies 10 m deep formed at Mount St. Helens within months, and old cones are cut into triangular facets (planezes) and keep only their radial symmetry [src](https://booksite.elsevier.com/9780126431407/netscape4/Contents/Chapt43_06.htm). Eroded scarps run 30–40°, "heavily gullied", with small talus at their bases [src](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/GG/FACULTY/ROWLAND/pdfs/Volcan_Ecuador_erosion.pdf).
- **Lava.** Aa is rough, jagged and clinkery, and looks thick and blocky; pahoehoe is smooth, billowy or ropy and advances in "toes" a few feet wide [src](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/lava-flow-forms.htm).
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: a smooth young cone or a gullied old one, the number of gullies, a lava tongue (blocky or lobed) running down into the land.

## 7. Sand: dunes and ripples (desert, coast)

- **Dunes.** The windward slope is gentle (under 15° on transverse dunes) and the slip face steep (about 32°). The forms are barchans with horns pointing downwind, transverse ridges across the wind, linear or seif dunes along it with knife-edged crests, and star dunes [src](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geography_%28Physical%29/The_Physical_Environment_%28Ritter%29/20%3A_Eolian_Systems/20.02%3A_Depositional_Forms).
- **Ripples** are "a few centimetres in wavelength", with crests "perpendicular to the prevailing wind", a stoss of 8–10°, a lee of 30–34° and a height of 1/8 to 1/22 of their length [src](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9910252). Their spacing is roughly constant in a steady wind [src](https://www.geocaching.com/geocache/GC876F7_erg-chebbi-ripple-marks).
- **How.** Shade the side away from the sun darker ("the stronger the light, the more marked the contrast"); the sunlit side can stay nearly flat. Draw "wavy and parallel furrows" on the nearest dune, sharp in its centre and fading toward its edges; give far dunes only gradients, outlined by a precise crest line. Add small rocks or grainy dots to break up flat areas [src](https://dessindigo.com/en/blog/drawing-tutorial/draw-desert). Sable treated its dunes as a "seascape" and fades its outlines with distance [src](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability).
- **Scale** [est, computed]. Ripple crests 5 cm apart that run across the view close to under 1 px beyond about 16 m; crests that point at the eye stay resolvable to about 160 m. Painted ripples belong only on the nearest band. Desert shrubs grow about 1.8–2.4 m apart [src](https://www.beg.utexas.edu/node/6269), which is still about 7 px across at 1 km, so scrub can stipple the far bands [est].
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: dune type, the wind's side (which face is dark), ripple spacing, scrub density, a gravel flat between dunes.

## 8. Snow and its shadows (mountains, tundra, mountain lake)

- **Value.** Sunlit snow is "shades of light grey", with "pure white reserved for highlights facing directly into the sun". Overcast light makes shadows greyer and lighter [src](https://theartistsroad.net/articles/winterpleinairpastel). Shadowed snow is "only slightly darker" than sunlit snow, and temperature does the work [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-techniques/winter-whites-painting-the-temperature-of-snow). Shadows painted too dark make snow look "dirty or even not like snow at all" [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/fr-fr/articles/10207?org=1).
- **Where it lies.** Snow collects in crevices, and "The sharp tips of rocks are typically snowless". Distant snow wants flat shading with slight gradients, because too many highlights read as shaved ice; the middle distance carries the weather [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/fr-fr/articles/10207?org=1). Sastrugi run parallel to the wind, and their small ripples stand about 10 mm high [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sastrugi).
- **Levitan's March.** It has blue shadows and relief strokes of up to two millimetres for the snow, most distinct in the foreground and more generalised toward the distance [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_%28painting%29).
- **Cast shadows** are sharp near what casts them, and lighter and softer farther away [src](https://evolveartist.com/blog/?p=4787). Shadows can mark where a forest edge stands [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/fr-fr/articles/10207?org=1).
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: drift direction, shadow length from the sun's height, rock breaking through, crust ridges, tree shadows laid across a band.

## 9. Tundra and heath (tundra)

- **From afar.** Tundra looks like a treeless plain broken up by patterned ground: stone polygons, soil circles, stone or soil stripes and terraces. "Polygonal patterns dominate flat surfaces", and vegetation keeps to their stable parts [src](https://people.ohio.edu/dyer/TPE/tpe_3e/biomes/biomes_tundra_arctic_page_1.html). Low-centred polygons have raised rims around wet basins, often in orthogonal networks, and thaw makes new ponds [src](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10611958-formation-low-centered-ice-wedge-polygons-orthogonal-systems-review).
- **How.** Ruskin's teacher Fielding crumbled burnt umber with a dry brush for foreground [src](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/ruskin/empi/notes/b185wham.htm). Scumbling drags light paint over dark with a dry brush, leaving the underlayer showing in flecks [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/scumbling/).
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: polygon size, ponds (sky-light patches), lichen and stone stipple, stripes on slopes, a heath of dry-brushed dark.

## 10. Still water (lake, still seeds)

- **Mirror.** Beaufort 0 is "Sea like a mirror" [src](https://www.weather.gov/ajk/beaufort). Reflections are duller than what they mirror, with darks often lighter and lights darker; distant water is flattened with a few short horizontal dashes, and its reflections shortened [src](https://www.creativebloq.com/features/10-tips-for-painting-vibrant-water). Reflection geometry and value are covered in painted-rivers.md (§3, §4).
- **Wind lines.** Pull a few horizontal lines out of the wet reflection with a damp flat brush, and angle them slightly near the bottom [src](https://onlineartlessons.com/?p=705). Ruskin was taught to paint calm lakes as broad strips of shade with lines of light [src](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/ruskin/empi/notes/b185wham.htm). Kensett adds gentle ripples and a thin line of light along the distant water [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/hudson-river-school-masterpieces/).
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: how many wind lines, where the mirror breaks, how long the reflections run.

## 11. Ruffled water: puffs, foam lines and shelter (ruffled seeds)

- **The look.** Beaufort 1 is scale-like ripples; at 2, crests are glassy and do not break [src](https://www.weather.gov/ajk/beaufort). Capillary ripples have a resonant wavelength of about 1.7 cm [src](https://bookdown.org/rbown250/sea_kayak_navigation/wind-waves.html). For cat's paws and slicks, see painted-rivers.md §5.
- **Puffs.** The leading edge of a gust is darker and more solid, an expanding oval; it then travels in a straight line. A small, very dark patch is a strong puff with defined edges; a lull is flat water with a darker line farther off as the next breeze comes [src](https://www.sailingworld.com/how-to/how-to-call-puffs-upwind-and-down/).
- **Foam lines.** Langmuir circulation draws "perfectly parallel lines of white foam" running with the wind, in a steady wind of under about 7 mph [src](https://blog.limnology.wisc.edu/2021/10/14/langmuir-circulation-explaining-lines-of-bubbles-on-the-surface-of-a-lake/).
- **Shelter.** A wind shadow is a thin strip of still water at the edges, a different colour from the rest [src](https://www.naturalnavigator.com/news/2022/02/what-is-a-wind-shadow/). On the shore the wind blows from, the water near is smooth and waves start farther out [src](https://mirjamglessmer.com/2016/05/17/walking-around-a-lake-to-look-at-waves-from-all-sides/). Waves grow downwind with fetch [src](https://bookdown.org/rbown250/sea_kayak_navigation/wind-waves.html).
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: wind direction, the number of puffs, a lull, the width of the sheltered strip, foam lines.

## 12. Choppy water and the open sea (choppy seeds, coast)

- **The look.** At Beaufort 3 crests begin to break; at 4 white horses are "fairly frequent", and at 5 "many" [src](https://www.weather.gov/ajk/beaufort). A fully developed sea at F3 is about 0.5 m with a 4 s period, and at F5 about 2.4 m with 8 s [src](https://bookdown.org/rbown250/sea_kayak_navigation/wind-waves.html). Large waves are steeper, 6–7% of their length against 3–4% for small ones [src](https://geo.libretexts.org/Courses/Sierra_College/Physical_Geology_%28Sierra_College_Edition%29/18%3A_Shorelines/18.01%3A_Waves).
- **How.** Make marks larger in the foreground and smaller and tighter as they recede, let parallel waves converge on a vanishing point, and soften the horizon [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-techniques/perspective/5-tips-for-more-depth-in-seascapes). One dominant wave works better than several, and a value study on seven steps keeps most of the sea mid-value [src](https://www.outdoorpainter.com/rising-tide-advice-for-painting-waves/). Limit the whites, keep foam subdued in front and in shadow, and use lost-and-found edges [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-techniques/insight-on-painting-seascapes). Paint waves as pointed ovals, tip, press, tip, varying spacing and tilt with the wind [src](https://www.creativebloq.com/features/10-tips-for-painting-vibrant-water).
- **Broken surface.** Choppy water suits broken colour, as in Monet's Etretat [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/broken-color/), and scumbling lighter paint over a broken base along the water's contours [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/scumbling/). The knife works rough water in several directions, with white on its edge for the crests [src](https://en.gallerix.ru/tech/ispolzovanie-mastixina-v-peyzazhnoy-zhivopisi).
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: a Beaufort number from 0 to 5, wave spacing and tilt, the count of white flecks, one dominant wave near.

## 13. A shore: breakers, foam and wet sand (coast)

- **Geometry.** Waves steepen in shallow water and refract until their crests run nearly parallel to the beach; off Long Beach, wavelength fell from about 100 m to about 50 m. Swash runs up the beach at an angle, and the backwash drains straight down the slope [src](https://geo.libretexts.org/Courses/Sierra_College/Physical_Geology_%28Sierra_College_Edition%29/18%3A_Shorelines/18.01%3A_Waves).
- **Foam.** Pounce the brush where the wave breaks, pull foam trails down the face with its side, and leave the dark underpainting showing in the foam. Use short, shaky level strokes where water settles at the rocks, and thin level lines of light on distant water and on wet sand [src](https://urartstudio.com/painting-ocean-wave-breaking-on-rocks-step-by-step-painting-guide/).
- **Wet sand** under a thin layer of water works like a mirror, and near the shore the water lightens as the sand shows through [src](https://paintingwithwatercolors.com/how-to-paint-water). Contrast the textures of sand and water, and bring the sand forward with a knife [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-techniques/perspective/5-tips-for-more-depth-in-seascapes).
- **What a seed could vary** [est]: two to four breaker lines, swash reach, the width of the wet-sand mirror, rocks in the surf.

## 14. Rapids and riffles (river valley; see painted-rivers.md)

- **How.** "Any lines you paint MUST be in the direction of the flow". Level lines mark ledges where the water pauses, white water goes on in impasto, and water spilling over a ledge catches the sky's reflection and reads lighter [src](https://onlineartlessons.com/?p=652). Show a drop, save whites, and add a weaker shade at each rock's base [src](https://www.wetcanvas.com/forums/topic/help-how-do-you-paint-moving-water). Homer scraped round patches about one centimetre across out of pale paint for the brightest foam [src](https://archive.artic.edu/homer/resource/600).

## 15. How texture changes with distance

- **Contrast.** Contrast "decreases exponentially with the distance" (Koschmieder), with 2% the usual threshold; visibility is 2–5 km in haze, 1–2 km in mist and under 1 km in fog [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visibility). With visual range V, the contrast left is exp(−3.9·d/V) [src](https://oceanopticsbook.info/view/photometry-and-visibility/level-2/contrast): 68% at d = V/10, 38% at V/4 and 14% at V/2 [est, computed]. Scattering also blurs and blues [src](https://ocw.uci.edu/upload/files/lecture022714.pdf).
- **Values compress, order stays.** "The value relationships will stay intact" as the range narrows. Flatten the texture and simplify the brushwork far off, and soften the edges. Where land overlaps, change the colour and brushwork more at each jump in space, more for a bigger jump [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/atmospheric-perspective/). Detail and texture become less distinct, and lines and shapes are softened [src](https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/Coalinga_College/Drawing_Perspectives/05%3A_Atmospheric_Perspective/5.02%3A_Key_Concepts-_Color_Modulation_Value_Changes_and_Reduction_in_Detail).
- **Marks.** Foreground marks are large, bold and impasto; far marks are small, thin and grey [src](https://www.virtualartacademy.com/?p=2210). "If the whole painting is equally sharp and equally textured, it can feel flat" [src](https://www.outdoorpainter.com/?p=41549). Thin paint goes behind and thick in front [src](https://www.wetcanvas.com/forums/topic/colors-in-the-foreground-middle-ground-and-background-for-grass). In Meier's renderer, larger, smoother strokes recede, while small textured ones come forward. The strokes are 2D in screen space, two or three layers per surface, jittered −10° to +20° [src](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall00/cs597b/papers/meier96.pdf).
- **Coarse to fine.** Hertzmann paints with brush radii of 8, 4 and 2 px and strokes 4–16 control points long. A finer layer is added only where the coarse one misses the source by more than a threshold, so detail lands only where the picture has it [src](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall00/cs597b/papers/hertzmann98.pdf).
- **Geometry** [est, computed]. A level eye sees a ground point at D = H/d. Upright things scale both ways as f/d and keep their aspect. A shape lying on the ground keeps its width f·s/d but its depth becomes f·s·H/d², a squash of D: 0.09 (1:11) at 5° below the horizon, 0.27 at 15°, 0.49 (1:2) at 26°. Over a frame whose land spans 1° to 26° below the horizon, mark size runs over a range of 28 to 1.

## 16. What we take

In priority order, each buildable in canvas 2D for a grey painting of horizontal ridge bands [est unless tagged].

1. **Depth per band.** Give each band a distance from its base line (as painted-rivers.md item 2), and from it a mark scale (1/d, clamped), a squash (D) and a contrast (exp(−3.9·d/V), with V set by the seed's haze). Every stroke kind reads all three.
2. **Upright marks and lying marks.** Grass, crowns and rock keep their aspect and only shrink. Field, snow, long, ripple, row and water marks are also squashed vertically by D. The far bands go level by themselves.
3. **Coarse to fine by depth.** Every band gets the coarse mark layer. The medium layer goes only nearer than about V/4, and the fine layer (seed heads, cracks, ripples, single blooms) only on the nearest two or three bands (§15).
4. **A second texture per band.** The seed gives each band its country's kind, plus a chance of one patch of another along the band's contour: a flower drift or a strip field in grassland, a clearing in forest, a scree apron below a cliff, an outcrop in desert. Variety then comes band by band, not only seed by seed.
5. **Carlson values.** Set the base value from the slope (flat floors lighter, steep faces darker, forest bands darkest). Let Kuindzhi's alternating lit and shadowed bands be one lighting plan the seed can choose [src](https://www.tretyakovgallerymagazine.com/node/6377).
6. **One wind per seed.** Its direction and strength set grass lean, honami, the dune's dark side, ripple and sastrugi direction, puffs and foam lines on water, so every texture agrees.
7. **Grass reworked.** Near bands get diagonal leaning strokes with short random marks at their tips (Van Gogh's heads). Far bands get a light-topped tone and few marks. Add one to three honami patches as soft, travelling lighter ellipses.
8. **Flower drifts.** Lay them along the band's ridge line, mostly within one value step of the grass, with a few stronger accents. Separate larger dabs go near, one soft patch far; the species sets light or dark.
9. **Rows.** A new row kind. Rows converge on a point of the horizon (lines from one point, so far fields keep them), or follow the band's own ridge profile (contour ploughing), in strips of two values. Furrows run darker and broader near.
10. **Canopy masses.** A crown kind: a bumpy top edge of round, spiky or umbrella silhouettes along the ridge, light-topped, with a darker base and two or three sky holes on near bands. Far forest bands are one value with a roughened edge, and jungle bands get mist between them.
11. **Rock strata and planes.** Mesas get level cliff-and-bench strata under a light caprock line. Cracks are dark lines with a light lip. Mountain faces are planes lit from the dial, and a concave scree apron at the foot gets coarser downslope.
12. **Dunes.** A sharp crest divides a flat lit windward slope from a darker slip face on the wind's lee. Wavy ripple lines run across the wind on the nearest band only, fading to its edges. Far dunes are gradients, and scrub dots are spaced ~2 m on the ground.
13. **Snow shadows.** Keep shadows one or two value steps below lit snow, never dark. Lay cast shadows of crowns and ridges across the band below, hard near the caster and softer away. Leave rock tips bare, and put level sastrugi strokes along the wind near.
14. **Tundra, volcano.** Tundra gets a squashed polygon net with pond lights and stipple, and stripes on slopes. A volcano gets radial gullies converging on the summit and a darker lava tongue with blocky marks.
15. **Water mood as Beaufort 0–5** [src](https://www.weather.gov/ajk/beaufort): mirror; scale dashes; glassy wavelets; broken crests with a few white flecks; frequent flecks and pointed-oval waves, larger near (§12).
16. **Wind on water.** On moods 1–3, add one to three dark puffs (very flat ovals far off), a lull where reflections return, a still mirror strip under the sheltering shore, and on light steady seeds two or three light foam lines along the wind (§11).
17. **Wind lines through reflections.** On still and ruffled seeds, cut the reflected far bands with a few level light lines, slightly tilted on near water [src](https://onlineartlessons.com/?p=705).
18. **A shore.** Draw two to four breaker lines parallel to the beach, closer toward land, each with pounced foam lace that lets the dark show. Add swash tongues up the sand, a wet-sand mirror band, and a thin light line on the far water.
19. **Rapids** on choppy river seeds: flow-aligned strokes, level light lines at ledges, round light flecks lifted with the knife around rocks, darker water at rock bases.

## 17. What this app can do that others don't

- **One wind for the whole world** [est]. A painter keeps grass, sand, snow and water consistent by care; here one seeded wind can drive all of them, and the moving image can let honami and puffs drift across the bands together.
- **Distance known exactly** [est]. Painters judge how much to simplify by eye. Every band here has a true distance, so squash, mark size and contrast can follow the geometry and Koschmieder's law, and the haze can be a single knob.
- **Impasto that the light turns** [est]. Levitan's 2 mm snow strokes [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_%28painting%29), knife ridges on rock and the dune's slip face can sit in the height map, so the light dial picks out the land's form, not only its paint.
- **A value study by design.** Church tested Twilight in the Wilderness in greyscale [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/hudson-river-school-masterpieces/); here the grey painting is the study, and Color only re-hues it. Carlson's planes and Kuindzhi's bands become rules the seed can pick between [est].
- **Water that knows its shores** [est]. The bands give each lake its shoreline and fetch, so sheltered mirror strips, wind lines and puffs can sit where the wind and land put them.

# Prior art: a river in a painted landscape

Research for Painted Landscape's river country ("green hills opening into a valley, the river winding down it toward you, widening as it comes"), after the artist's note that the river "needs work". Today it reads as one flat, even, bright ribbon running nearly straight from the horizon (often under the sun) to the foot of the frame, stepped where the ridge bands meet: a road or a sunbeam, not water.

**Tags.** [src](URL) marks a claim from a source opened and read for this note. [est] marks an estimate, a computation or a design judgement. W is the river's width, H the eye's height above the water, and angles are measured below (or above) the horizon unless marked.

## Summary

- **Scale.** Real rivers bend to a scale set by their own width: a meander is 10–14 widths long (11 on average), its bends have a radius of 2–3 widths, and channels run 1.3 to 4 times longer than their valleys [src](https://solarspell-dls.sfis.asu.edu/mea/wikipedia/wp/m/Meander.htm) [src](https://facultyweb.kennesaw.edu/jdirnber/docs/Leopold%20Riv%20Meand%201966.pdf). Each bend has sand on its inside and a steep bank with the deepest water on its outside [src](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geography_%28Physical%29/The_Environment_of_the_Earth%27s_Surface_%28Southard%29/05%3A_Rivers/5.09%3A_Morphology_and_Dynamics_of_Meandering_Streams).
- **Perspective.** Perspective shapes the river more than its bends do. Within about 5° of the horizon, a whole meander is foreshortened to less than the river's own width, whatever the camera, so the far river is a stack of thin, level slivers and only the near reaches can run diagonally down the frame [est, computed, §2]. Painters narrow the S-curve as it recedes [src](https://members.drawpaintacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Common-Landscape-Compositions.pdf), and warn that too many rounded curves make it "look too much like a waterfall or hiking path" [src](https://www.outdoorpainter.com/why-this-works-spring-renewal/).
- **Value.** The angle sets the value of the water. Flat water reflects about 2% of the light looking straight down and almost all of it at grazing angles [src](https://scubageek.com/articles/wwwatw). Computed: 8% at 26° below the horizon, 24% at 14°, 58% at 5° and 86% at 1.4° [est, §3]. Painters paint water "very light where its further away and dark in the foreground" [src](https://archives.sundayobserver.lk/2014/03/16/mon04.asp).
- **Reflections** drop strictly vertically and keep depth order, and they are cut short for anything set back from the edge [src](https://eclecticlight.co/2015/11/30/reflections-in-art-2-how-to-paint-perfect-reflections-on-water/). Light things reflect a little darker and dark things a little lighter, and every reflection comes back softer and duller [src](https://theartistsroad.net/articles/animperfectmirror).
- **Ripples and glitter.** Ripples foreshorten into horizontal striations [src](https://atoptics.co.uk/fz236.htm). A gust (a cat's paw) darkens the water and an oily slick lightens it [src](https://blog.kootenay-lake.ca/?p=9678) [src](https://www.naturalnavigator.com/news/2019/01/what-is-a-slick-line/). Sun glitter lies only in the vertical plane through the sun and the eye: its length is 4 × the steepest wave slope, and its width over length is the sine of the sun's elevation [src](https://www.montana.edu/jshaw/documents/Glitter_patterns_OPN1999.pdf).
- **Edges.** Painters' signatures at the edge are a dark line under the bank and a thin light line where water meets shore [src](https://urartstudio.com/step-by-step-painting-instructions/step-5-creating-shoreline-and-water-ripples/) [src](https://dessindigo.com/en/blog/drawing-tutorial/how-to-draw-a-river). Trees outlining a stream "stand out above farm fields and pastures" [src](https://geokansas.ku.edu/riparian-zones).

## 1. How a real river bends

- **Scale.** A meander is 10–14 bankfull widths long, 11 on average. It is 3–5 (mean 4.7) times the radius of curvature at its apex, and that radius is 2–3 widths [src](https://solarspell-dls.sfis.asu.edu/mea/wikipedia/wp/m/Meander.htm). Leopold and Langbein's two examples both have a radius of about 2.3 widths; the gentle, sine-like meander is about 11.5 widths long and the tight loop about 6.9 [src](https://facultyweb.kennesaw.edu/jdirnber/docs/Leopold%20Riv%20Meand%201966.pdf).
- **Sinuosity** (channel length over valley length). A channel at 1–1.5 is sinuous and at 1.5–4 meandering; measure it over at least 20 widths [src](https://solarspell-dls.sfis.asu.edu/mea/wikipedia/wp/m/Meander.htm). Leopold's sample ran from 1.3 to 4 [src](https://facultyweb.kennesaw.edu/jdirnber/docs/Leopold%20Riv%20Meand%201966.pdf).
- **The curve.** The channel's direction is "a sine function of the distance measured along the channel": the deflection is zero at the axis of a bend and greatest at the inflections. Of the curves compared, it has the least total change of direction: 3,940, against 4,840 for circular arcs and 5,200 for a sine wave [src](https://facultyweb.kennesaw.edu/jdirnber/docs/Leopold%20Riv%20Meand%201966.pdf). That is why a plain sine wave of x against depth looks wrong. With a peak deflection ω, the sinuosity is 1/J0(ω): 1.2 at 50°, 1.5 at 70° and 2.1 at 90° [est, computed].
- **The bed.** Pools lie at the bend apexes, and shallow riffles at the crossings, spaced 5–7 widths apart [src](https://facultyweb.kennesaw.edu/jdirnber/docs/Leopold%20Riv%20Meand%201966.pdf). The deepest line (the thalweg) hugs the outer bank and crosses back over the riffles [src](https://solarspell-dls.sfis.asu.edu/mea/wikipedia/wp/m/Meander.htm). "The flow is deepest near the foot of the eroding outer bank, and it shoals gradually up the point-bar surface" [src](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geography_%28Physical%29/The_Environment_of_the_Earth%27s_Surface_%28Southard%29/05%3A_Rivers/5.09%3A_Morphology_and_Dynamics_of_Meandering_Streams).
- **The banks** [src](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geography_%28Physical%29/The_Environment_of_the_Earth%27s_Surface_%28Southard%29/05%3A_Rivers/5.09%3A_Morphology_and_Dynamics_of_Meandering_Streams). Outside each bend is a steep cut bank, undercut low and collapsing into the channel. Inside is the point bar, "a fairly smooth and largely non-vegetated body of sand, sloping gently downward toward the center of the channel". Curving meander scrolls mark the floodplain inside the bends. Abandoned loops plug up into oxbow lakes, which stay visible as differences in colour and vegetation. Bends grow toward larger amplitudes and narrower necks until a flood cuts across.
- **The valley.** In a young V-shaped valley, spurs project "alternately from the opposite sides". Each one extends "into a concave bend of the river", and seen down the valley they stagger "like the teeth of a zipper" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlocking_spur).
- **Widening downstream.** Width grows roughly as the square root of discharge: Leopold and Maddock's average exponent is b = 0.5, and across 72 rivers it ranged from 0.03 to 0.89, most often 0.4–0.5 [src](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1982/0436/report.pdf). Over the few kilometres a painting shows, the river barely widens, so its "widening as it comes" is nearly all perspective [est].
- **Trees.** Trees along streams outline their routes and "stand out above farm fields and pastures" [src](https://geokansas.ku.edu/riparian-zones). A planted buffer is at least 33 ft (10 m) of trees and shrubs (cottonwood, poplar, willow, silver maple), with grass outside it [src](https://naturalresources.extension.iastate.edu/forestry/planning/buffer.html).

## 2. A river seen down its valley

**What painters do**

- **The S-shape** "starts with a wide opening then gets progressively narrower" (Streeton, The River, 1896). It is built from things already there, such as a shoreline or a tree line, and kept subtle: "People do not like to feel they are being pushed through your painting" [src](https://members.drawpaintacademy.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Common-Landscape-Compositions.pdf).
- **Foreshortening.** "A river winding into the distance" is a named case of strong foreshortening. In Levitan's Silent Abode, the bridge across the river is squashed while the far shoreline keeps its full length [src](https://drawpaintacademy.com/?p=14998).
- **Converging banks.** The two banks converge on one point of the horizon and spread apart lower down, and the curves follow one another consistently [src](https://dessindigo.com/en/blog/drawing-tutorial/how-to-draw-a-river). A road that disappears and re-emerges in the distance looks "much thinner and smaller" [src](https://www.paintingclass.net/descending-perspective-falling-river/).
- **Level lines.** On a stream painting, horizontal lines "step you back gradually" and "Level water takes the eye around bends". The warning is about too many rounded curves, which make it look like a waterfall or a hiking path [src](https://www.outdoorpainter.com/why-this-works-spring-renewal/). That is the current river's fault.
- **Mist.** In Guo Xi's Old Trees, Level Distance, the mountains beyond the river valley are blurred to suggest mist [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Trees,_Level_Distance).

**The geometry** [est, computed]. The camera is level and the water a flat plane H below the eye. D is depth below the horizon, in focal lengths (D = tan of the angle down).

- **Width and swing.** A point at ground distance d sits at D = H/d. On screen, the river's width is W·D/H, and a sideways offset X appears as X·D/H. So the river swings by the same number of its own widths at every depth, and on a straight reach its edges are straight lines running to the vanishing point.
- **Depth compresses as D².** One meander length λ takes up λ·D²/H of height, which is (λ/W)·D river widths. With λ = 11W, a whole meander is shallower on screen than the river is wide wherever D < 0.09, that is within 5° of the horizon. Tight loops (λ ≈ 7W) collapse within 8°.
- **Reaches flatten.** A reach at angle θ to the line of sight runs tanθ/D across for each unit down. At 5° below the horizon, a 30° reach runs 6.6 : 1, nearly level. At the frame's foot (about 26° below for a horizon at 47% of the height and a 50° vertical field, D = 0.49), the same reach runs 1.2 : 1, a diagonal. So the top fifth of the land shows the river only as level slivers, and only its lower half can run steeply.
- **Hidden reaches.** A ridge or spur hides every reach behind it. A spur reaching across the valley hides a stretch of the river, which reappears thinner beyond it (as the interlocking spurs and the road above show).

## 3. The value of water: angle, depth and the sky

- **The optics.** Water reflects about 2% of light at normal incidence. Near Brewster's angle (about 53°) the parallel component nearly vanishes, and at grazing incidence both components are "almost completely reflected" [src](https://scubageek.com/articles/wwwatw). These shares are computed from the Fresnel equations for flat water with n = 1.34 [src](https://oceanopticsbook.info/view/surfaces) [est, computed]:

  | Angle below the horizon | 26° | 20° | 14° | 10° | 7° | 5° | 3° | 1.4° | 0.6° |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Share of the sky reflected | 8% | 13% | 24% | 36% | 48% | 58% | 74% | 86% | 94% |

  Schlick's form, R ≈ 0.02 + 0.98·(1 − sin δ)^5, fits these values within about 6 points [est].
- **Mirror geometry.** Flat water at δ below the horizon reflects whatever stands at δ above it. In a level picture, for things far beyond the water, that is the picture mirrored about the horizon line [est, from the vertical-mirror rule in §4].
- **Light far, dark near.** The water is "very light where its further away and dark in the foreground", because it mirrors the lighter low sky; foreground water "transmits some of the colours of the bottom", because we look down into it [src](https://archives.sundayobserver.lk/2014/03/16/mon04.asp). Foreground water is usually darker because it reflects the darker sky nearly overhead, and near the shore it is shallow, so more of the dark bottom shows [src](https://theartistsroad.net/articles/animperfectmirror).
- **Bottom and surface.** Looking straight down you see the bottom; by the back of the water, the surface "is entirely covered by reflection". Under water, the range of values is narrower than above it [src](https://www.paintingclass.net/translucence-and-reflection/).
- **Darker than the sky.** Sky and water share colours, "although the water will be darker in value" [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-subjects/plein-air/the-art-of-painting-water-4-tips). One watercolourist lays the river in "a slightly stronger tone of the sky colour", "strengthened in tone and colour as the river moved towards the bottom of the sheet" [src](https://paintingwithwatercolors.com/painting-the-river-and-reflections-with-watercolor).
- **The exception.** On a sea with waves, the distance can turn darker: near waves show their far slopes, which reflect the light low sky, while distant waves reflect higher, darker sky [src](https://www.wetcanvas.com/forums/topic/rules-of-the-atmospheric-perspective-in-sea-or-ocean). A river's ripples are small, so the order above holds for it [est].

## 4. Reflections

- **Geometry.** "A line joining any point on the original with its equivalent on the reflection will be strictly vertical." Left and right stay the same, "reflections preserve depth order", and because the reflection is seen from below it shows undersides and different highlights [src](https://eclecticlight.co/2015/11/30/reflections-in-art-2-how-to-paint-perfect-reflections-on-water/). It can even show things the eye can't see directly [src](https://streamlinepublishing.com/inside-art/some-reflections-on-painting-water/).
- **Things set back from the edge.** "The further back from the water's edge that an object is situated, the more truncated is its reflection", and a house up a hill may have none at all [src](https://eclecticlight.co/2015/11/30/reflections-in-art-2-how-to-paint-perfect-reflections-on-water/). To construct one, extend the water under the object, reflect it there, then trim to the real edge [src](https://eclecticlight.co/2023/05/24/hard-reality-6-reflections-in-the-landscape/).
- **Length and breaks.** On smooth water a reflection is as tall as its object, and undulations can stretch it longer. Ripples leave gaps, and choppy distant water may show no reflection at all [src](https://paintingwithwatercolors.com/2023/07/painting-reflections-ultimate-guide).
- **Value.**
  - Light tones "will appear a little darker in the reflection" [src](https://jamesgurney.substack.com/p/water-reflections).
  - Dark ones may come back lighter: "Not all of the darkness is reflected either". Reflected colours are less saturated, and small details are lost [src](https://theartistsroad.net/articles/animperfectmirror).
  - "dark objects reflect lighter, light objects reflect darker", and "The water always softens what it mirrors" [src](https://watercolourworkout.substack.com/p/two-for-one).
  - Wind often lightens the darkness of a reflection [src](https://paintingwithwatercolors.com/2023/07/painting-reflections-ultimate-guide).
- **Distance.** Near reflections come out "warmer and more distinct", far ones "bluer and less distinct" [src](https://paintingwithwatercolors.com/painting-the-river-and-reflections-with-watercolor). The sky shows through gaps in reflected trees [src](https://paintingwithwatercolors.com/2023/07/painting-reflections-ultimate-guide).
- **Handling.** Paint still reflections "as a large tonal mass of color, rather than a lot of individual strokes" [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-subjects/plein-air/the-art-of-painting-water-4-tips). Bob Ross carries the land's dark into the water, then pulls "the reflections straight down, then gently brush across" [src](https://createtv.com/CreateProgram.nsf/vInstructions/Bob%20Ross%27%20Mountain%20Reflections%20Painting).

## 5. Ripples, wind and the sun's glitter

- **Striations.** Foreshortening "makes undulations look like horizontal striations", even when the waves are not running toward you [src](https://atoptics.co.uk/fz236.htm).
- **A cat's paw** is "a dark (occasionally lighter) patch". Its steep capillary ripples (wavelength under 1.7 cm) tip the surface until it "takes on some of the darkness of looking straight in". It leaves no trail and dies out within metres [src](https://blog.kootenay-lake.ca/?p=9678).
- **A slick** forms where a film of oil flattens the ripples, so "more of the sky gets reflected" and it reads lighter. Wind gathers slicks into long, narrow streaks [src](https://www.naturalnavigator.com/news/2019/01/what-is-a-slick-line/).
- **Glitter: its size** [src](https://www.montana.edu/jshaw/documents/Glitter_patterns_OPN1999.pdf) [src](https://psl.noaa.gov/outreach/education/science/glitter). Each glint is a facet tilted to mirror the sun. The pattern's length is 4 × the maximum wave slope, and its width over length is the sine of the sun's elevation: round under a high sun, a narrow ellipse under a low one. It is narrowest at an elevation of twice the maximum slope; lower than that, shadowing shortens it. The mean-square slope rises roughly linearly with wind speed.
- **Glitter: where it lies.** The path lies "at the intersection between the horizontal plane of the water and a vertical plane" through the sun and the eye. Still water shows only the mirrored disc, and gentle waves give a narrow path, brightest at the mirror point [src](https://atoptics.co.uk/fz236.htm). Glint needs ripples: where the water is smoother, it disappears [src](https://cimss.ssec.wisc.edu/wxwise/class/picture0.html). For example, ripples up to 5° and a sun 15° up give a path about 20° long, centred 15° below the horizon and about 5° wide [est].
- **Pillars.** In Kuchel's paper, the pillars that lights make over rippled water have nearly parallel sides: the band of facets that mirror the light widens with distance about as fast as perspective shrinks it [src](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.18547).

## 6. Banks and edges

- **Dark line, then light line.** Tap a dark line in "just below the line of our distant tree formation", then make "horizontal strokes to separate the shoreline from the water", keeping them parallel. "Only a few well-placed lines are necessary to achieve a calm water effect" [src](https://urartstudio.com/step-by-step-painting-instructions/step-5-creating-shoreline-and-water-ripples/).
- **Dessindigo.** Shade the bank more, "then add a thin white line along the edge between river and bank". Lay strokes perpendicular to the flow, denser in the shadow near the banks. Take the water's value from the sky, with touches of the ground and trees near the banks [src](https://dessindigo.com/en/blog/drawing-tutorial/how-to-draw-a-river).
- **Bob Ross** cuts water lines and ripples with Liquid White on the knife's long edge [src](https://createtv.com/CreateProgram.nsf/vInstructions/Bob%20Ross%27%20Basic%20Landscape%20Painting). He lays banks in Van Dyke Brown and Dark Sienna with the knife, then highlights them [src](https://www.quarto.com/public/dynamic/files/uploadfile/2022/11/20221104153218395_Painting_with_Bob_Ross___Around_the_Bend.pdf).
- **Watercolour.** Highlights are "scratched into the water line" [src](https://paintingwithwatercolors.com/painting-the-river-and-reflections-with-watercolor). The distant water is left dry "to create some sparkle", and the brush is dragged sideways across it [src](https://www.artsupplies.co.uk/blog/bridge-over-the-river-usk-south-wales-watercolour-tutorial/).
- **Gouache.** The foreground bank goes in darker greens with browns, "adding more contrast and shadow". "The grassy banks act as leading lines" [src](https://finearttutorials.com/guide/how-to-paint-a-landscape-with-gouache/).
- **Games.** "a Ghibli-style stream typically has very thin foam near the shore" [src](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/nature-shaders-with-shader-graph-in-2022-lts).
- **Which bank shows what** [est]. Seen from above, a reach's far bank shows its face to you, with that face's reflection right below it. Its near bank shows only its top, ending at the water in a shadowed lip.

## 7. How painters and games simplify

- **Kazuo Oga** thins his poster colour until its gradations look like transparent watercolour, and works with about 21 colours: "The look came from the contrasts, the combinations". He extracts a scene's essence, and on site watched mist rise from valleys [src](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/what-kazuo-oga-thinks-about-when).
- **Poster colour** was "used for background paintings in Japanese animation", and mixes wet-in-wet washes with opaque brushwork [src](https://lightboxexpo.com/past-presentation/painting-an-anime-style-background-in-gouache/). Oga works "from the large details in toward the small" [src](https://www.openculture.com/2021/01/a-look-inside-the-painting-process-of-the-studio-ghibli-artist-kazuo-oga.html).
- **Firewatch** builds its palette in "very distinct layers", "flat-shapes with strong, distinctive silhouettes with abstract internal details", with fog for depth [src](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch). The layers lighten toward the horizon and were worked out in black and white first [src](https://blogs.ulster.ac.uk/bethbuchanan/2021/03/05/ixd104-further-looking-into-the-art-of-firewatch).
- **Sable** fades its outlines with distance, which reinforces perspective [src](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability).
- **Alto's Odyssey** asks "how far can you simplify a form until you're left only with its essence" [src](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-team-alto-s-i-alto-s-odyssey-i-). Its backgrounds are muted to set them apart [src](https://www.dice.com/career-advice/altos-odyssey-winning-mobile-game).
- **Economy.** Leave much of the paper bare and add "a few telling ripples and reflections" [src](https://archives.sundayobserver.lk/2014/03/16/mon04.asp). A few opaque white strokes for ripples: "This is what makes the surface read as water" [src](https://watercolourworkout.substack.com/p/two-for-one).

## 8. What makes a painted river read as water

1. **The water carries its own gradient.** It is light far off and darker near, never one tone, and darker than the sky except where it glitters (§3) [src](https://archives.sundayobserver.lk/2014/03/16/mon04.asp).
2. **Bends flatten toward the horizon.** They become level slivers, and the river vanishes behind spurs and comes back thinner (§2) [est] [src](https://www.paintingclass.net/descending-perspective-falling-river/).
3. **Reflections hang straight down from the far bank.** They are darker than the sky, softer than what they mirror, and gapped where the sky shows (§4) [src](https://eclecticlight.co/2015/11/30/reflections-in-art-2-how-to-paint-perfect-reflections-on-water/).
4. **Every mark is horizontal:** ripples, streaks, water lines (§5) [src](https://atoptics.co.uk/fz236.htm).
5. **The edges are lined:** a thin light line where water meets the far shore, and a dark line under the bank (§6) [src](https://urartstudio.com/step-by-step-painting-instructions/step-5-creating-shoreline-and-water-ripples/).
6. **Bends are asymmetric:** pale sand inside, a steep bank and the deepest, darkest water outside (§1) [src](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geography_%28Physical%29/The_Environment_of_the_Earth%27s_Surface_%28Southard%29/05%3A_Rivers/5.09%3A_Morphology_and_Dynamics_of_Meandering_Streams).
7. **Trees trace the banks,** so the river's course reads even where its water is hidden [src](https://geokansas.ku.edu/riparian-zones).
8. **Glitter appears only at the sun's mirror point,** on the bits of river that cross its column; it is never the whole river [src](https://atoptics.co.uk/fz236.htm).
9. **The river is uneven.** An even width and value with smooth, rounded curves is what makes a road [src](https://www.outdoorpainter.com/why-this-works-spring-renewal/).

## 9. What we take

In priority order, each buildable in canvas 2D for a grey painting of horizontal ridge bands [est unless tagged].

1. **One river, projected.** Lay the centreline on the ground as a sine-generated curve in distance: λ ≈ 11W, peak deflection 60–90°. Project every point with y = horizon + f·H/d, x = centre + f·X/d and half-width f·W/(2d). Draw that one polygon; never draw a separate reach per band. The steps where bands meet go away, and the far bends flatten into slivers by themselves (§1, §2).
2. **Bands at their distances.** Give each band a distance from its base, d = f·H/(base − horizon). After laying band i in, repaint the river polygon for every depth nearer than d_i. The next band covers what it hides, and the pieces meet exactly.
3. **Spurs, not notches.** Stop carving a symmetric dip over the river in every band. In one band in two, end the ridge as a spur that overlaps the river by 1–2 widths from alternate sides, so the river vanishes and re-emerges thinner [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlocking_spur).
4. **The value by angle.** Make the water value R·(reflected value) + (1 − R)·(body value), with R from the table in §3 or Schlick's form by each point's angle below the horizon. Make the body about as dark as the darkest near land; that yields a light far sliver and a mid-dark near reach.
5. **Mirror the picture into it.** Inside a river clip, draw the painted sky flipped about the horizon. Then draw each farther band's profile flipped about that band's own floor line, pulled about 10% toward mid grey (light darker, dark lighter) and softened with distance. Truncate the reflection of anything set back from the edge (§4).
6. **Water lines.** Along each reach's far edge, run a hairline about 1 canonical px wide (wider near), 15–25% lighter than the water. Under the near bank's lip, run a dark line about twice that width. Both stay strictly horizontal on far reaches and follow the bank near (§6).
7. **Point bars and cut banks.** On the inside of each bend, from the inflection to past the apex, lay a light crescent of sand up to half a width wide with a soft edge toward the water. On the outside, give the bank a steep dark face and put the deepest, darkest water along it (§1).
8. **A tree line on the banks.** Cluster bushes and trees along both banks within about 1–3 widths, rather than keeping them away from the river. The river's course then reads behind the spurs [src](https://geokansas.ku.edu/riparian-zones).
9. **Strokes by distance.** All ripple strokes are horizontal, and their length and spacing grow with D. In the bright far water they are slightly darker streaks; in the dark near water they are light streaks that catch the low sky [src](https://www.wetcanvas.com/forums/topic/rules-of-the-atmospheric-perspective-in-sea-or-ocean). The seed's mood sets how many.
10. **Wind patches.** On ruffled seeds, add one to three cat's paws (soft dark ovals, very wide and flat in the distance) and long light slick streaks. On still seeds, add neither [src](https://blog.kootenay-lake.ca/?p=9678) [src](https://www.naturalnavigator.com/news/2019/01/what-is-a-slick-line/).
11. **Glitter only where the river crosses the sun's column.** Its x lies within about ± length·sin(elevation)/2 of the sun, and its depth within about ± twice the maximum slope of the sun's mirror point. Choose the river's phase so its near, steep reach does not sit under the sun (§5).
12. **Width.** Let the true width grow only 20–50% over the whole view, and let perspective do the widening. Choose W/H so the nearest reach spans about 15–30% of the frame (§1, §2).
13. **Riffles on choppy seeds.** Add short broken light dashes across the river at each crossing between bends, 5–7 widths apart [src](https://facultyweb.kennesaw.edu/jdirnber/docs/Leopold%20Riv%20Meand%201966.pdf).
14. **Rare seeds.** Add an oxbow (a still, mirror-smooth crescent off the main channel) or a few faint meander-scroll arcs on the valley floor [src](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Geography_%28Physical%29/The_Environment_of_the_Earth%27s_Surface_%28Southard%29/05%3A_Rivers/5.09%3A_Morphology_and_Dynamics_of_Meandering_Streams).

## 10. What this app can do that others don't

- **True reflections, cheaply** [est]. The painting has its own sky and its own ridge bands as layers, so the river can mirror exactly what was painted at any time of day. A painter has to guess this, and a game needs a second render.
- **A path of values** [est]. In a grey picture, the river can carry the lead-in by value alone: from the far sliver nearly as light as the low sky, down to the dark near reach. Mapped to the page's two colours, it reads as the page's own light. Its far reaches could be aimed at where the poem sits.
- **The river holds the last light** [est, from §3]. After sunset the low sky stays bright while the land goes dark, and at grazing angles the river reflects up to 90% of that sky. So at dusk the river becomes the brightest shape on the land; at night it can hold a moon's glitter in one bend.
- **Weather the eye can follow** [est]. The seed's water mood can become a wind field: cat's paws drifting in a moving image, slicks lying along the valley, glitter breaking up as a gust crosses the sun's column.
- **Lit by the engine** [est]. If the landscape goes to the light engine, the river is the one place in the painting where gloss should be full and the height flat, so the engine's own specular finds it.

# The Ringworld's Trees: Conifers, Variety, Volume and Light Through Leaves

**What Ruby asked (2026-10-22).** "Change the conifers; make sure all the trees match the current art style and none have disconnected pieces; trees should have variations in size and form within species too, they don't all look the same; not just colour, minor colour variation but actual shape and branch variations; shading on all trees; texture when close to camera; leaf translucency when close to camera; leaf subsurface scattering (you can fake it) when close."

Scope: pkTree in paintKit.js, a canvas-2D painter of flat-shaded, faceted sprites (polygons, ellipses, strokes) from a height in px, four colours (trunk, dark, body, lit), a sun side and the light dial; shaded from the sky down, with a cast shadow (docs/prior-art/ringworld-sprites.md). Tagging: [src](URL) is a page opened and read for this note; [est] is my own estimate or inference, to be tuned by eye.

**What is wrong now, from reading paintKit.js [est].**
- The Scots pine's crown floats: its trunk stops at 0.62 of the height and its two branch strokes end by 0.63, but the squashed crown's lowest edge sits at about 0.70 to 0.74, a gap of 0.06 to 0.11 of the tree's height [est].
- The near stone pine (over 30 px) builds its dome as an arch of lobes: the middle lobes' undersides sit about 0.12 to 0.2 of the height above the branch tips, so sky shows under the dome's centre [est].
- A small poplar is three balls with gaps of 0.1 and 0.17 of the height between them, and a birch's seven random lobes can land up to 0.14 of the height from the trunk with nothing joining them [est].
- Spruce and fir are one zigzag polygon, mirrored left and right, with a lit half and lit triangles: a stack of triangles. Every spruce differs only in 5 to 7 tiers and a base width of 0.20 to 0.26 of its height [est].
- Palms, bamboo, ferns, dead trees and saguaros get a lit side but no sky-down ramp, no occlusion and no near texture; nothing anywhere draws light through leaves [est].

## 1. Conifer forms by species

**What makes a conifer read as a conifer and not as stacked triangles.**
- The silhouette comes from where the branches point: up, level, scooping or draping, and each species has its own [src](https://www.unisoncolour.com/blog/combating-cookie-cutter-conifers-by-tracey-maras/). The angle and spread of the limbs and the shape of the trunk vary greatly between species [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-mediums/drawing/convincing-conifers-how-to-draw-trees).
- "Not all of the branches extend to the outer edge of the crown" [src](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-mediums/drawing/convincing-conifers-how-to-draw-trees). Needles sit mostly at the ends of the branches [src](https://www.unisoncolour.com/blog/combating-cookie-cutter-conifers-by-tracey-maras/). So the edge is a row of separate branch tips, not a straight cone line [est].
- Sky holes, where sky colour peeks through, give light and air, and they are commonest round the edge [src](https://www.unisoncolour.com/blog/combating-cookie-cutter-conifers-by-tracey-maras/). Paint "gaps where the light shines through", and keep most lines jagged so they suggest branches [src](https://etchrlab.com/blogs/news/drawing-and-painting-pine-trees).
- The trunk is a strong, tapering leader, sometimes only hinted at [src](https://www.unisoncolour.com/blog/combating-cookie-cutter-conifers-by-tracey-maras/). Draw it to the tip and let it show in the gaps between tiers, so every tier visibly hangs from it [est].
- The innermost part of a pine is darkest and the needles sticking out catch the light [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/zh-tw/articles/7585?org=1). The darkest values are in shadow and low down; the upper foliage is lighter and warmer [src](https://www.unisoncolour.com/blog/combating-cookie-cutter-conifers-by-tracey-maras/).
- Density is a species trait. A fir is stiff and compact with "less air showing through"; a spruce is "more relaxed and open"; a hemlock droops at every tip [src](https://sibleyguides.com/2011/02/spruce-fir-or-hemlock).
- Branches grow from the trunk in regular whorls [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce). On young trees each whorl is one year's growth [src](https://bio.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Botany/Forest_Measurements_-_An_Applied_Approach_(DeYoung)/04%3A_Tree_Age/04.3%3A_Field_Technique_Tips_for_Counting_Whorls%3A). So a tier is a layer of branches with a visible step between layers, not a triangle [est].
- Jane Ng called pines "the worst" tree to make in 3D [src](https://80.lv/articles/creating-the-world-of-firewatch). Their look is in the branch structure, which is exactly what flat triangles throw away [est].

**Age, exposure and gaps.**
- Krummholz is "stunted, deformed vegetation" at the tree line [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krummholz). On a flag tree the branches on the windward side die, so the crown looks like a flag [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krummholz). "On the leeward side, the trunk shelters the branches," so they grow to normal size [src](https://www.weathergamut.com/2011/12/15/flag-trees/).
- The windward side is "scoured away, and the tree grows to the leeward" [src](https://nwt.colorado.edu/field_trip/krummholz.html). The zone of abrasion reaches "nearly a meter above the snow's surface", tree islands spread parallel to the wind, and badly damaged trees "creep along the ground in gnarled mats" [src](https://churchillscience.ca/tree-island/). So draw a dense skirt below about 1 m, a bare band, and a one-sided flag above [est].
- Trees at a forest edge "usually develop a bowed stem and asymmetric crown" [src](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/forestry/how-tree-growth-affects-tree-failure/). A crown can be "unbalanced, lopsided, or developed as a flag" [src](https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/205/301/ic/cdc/environmental/environment/forest/crown.htm).
- Old Douglas firs have an irregular live crown, a dead, damaged or broken top, and large horizontal branches in their upper half [src](https://scmbc.org/characteristics-of-old-growth-douglas-firs).
- Dropped lower branches leave "stubs and/or knots" on the trunk [src](https://bio.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Botany/Forest_Measurements_-_An_Applied_Approach_(DeYoung)/04%3A_Tree_Age/04.3%3A_Field_Technique_Tips_for_Counting_Whorls%3A). Draw 0–4 grey dead stubs below the live crown on forest-grown trees [est].
- A young Norway spruce can grow 1 m a year for 25 years [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picea_abies), so a 25 m tree carries 25–50 whorls. Paint 6–12 tiers, each standing for 3–5 whorls, as painters do when they build the limbs downward, "covering more horizontal width" as they go [src](https://www.twoinchbrush.com/articles/evergreens) [est].
- Foresters measure the green crown as 100·l/h (live crown length over height) and model conifer crowns as cones [src](https://fennerschool-associated.anu.edu.au/mensuration/BrackandWood1998/CROWN.HTM). Use a live crown of 0.8–1.0 for open-grown trees and 0.3–0.5 in forest [est].

## 2. Broadleaf forms and their variation within one species

- **Open-grown trees** have "a short trunk with a massive, low-branching, wide-spreading crown". **Forest-grown trees** shed their lower branches and grow "a clear, vertical stem" [src](https://gabrielhemery.com/knowledge/topics/tree-architecture-and-growth-forms/). Open-grown trees are squat with a large crown, and forest trees spindly [src](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/forestry/how-tree-growth-affects-tree-failure/).
- English oak: "massive, broadly rounded, open-headed ... with a short trunk; in youth pyramidal to rounded", 75–100 ft "with a comparable spread", so W:H about 1.0 when open-grown [src](https://cornellbotanicgardens.org/plant/english-oak). It has "a broad, spreading crown above thick branches", 20–40 m [src](https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/node/603). Forest oaks run 0.5–0.7 [est].
- Beech grows "a huge domed crown" over 40 m [src](https://woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/british-trees/a-z-of-british-trees/common-beech). Silver birch has "a light canopy with elegant, drooping branches" to 30 m [src](https://woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/british-trees/a-z-of-british-trees/silver-birch/).
- **Crown versus trunk.** Open-grown oak, ash, sycamore and beech have crowns about 2.5 m across at 0.1 m trunk diameter and 12–14 m at 0.7 m [src](https://gabrielhemery.com/how-big-will-this-tree-grow), so the crown is 19–25 times the trunk's width. Use trunk width = crown width / 20, ±20% [est].
- **Asymmetry and lean.** At the edges of logging gaps, 83% of 192 crowns were wider on the gap side, by 2.1 m on average, and crown asymmetry may stimulate bole curvature [src](https://jtfs.frim.gov.my/jtfs/article/view/2825). On a 10–15 m crown that is a 7–10% shift toward the open side [est].
- **Forks.** Decurrent trees (oak, hickory, maple) have weak apical dominance and end in "a rounded or spreading tree crown" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decurrent); "all trees begin with an excurrent form when they are young" [src](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/forestry/how-tree-growth-affects-tree-failure/). So a mature broadleaf trunk forks into 2–4 limbs at 0.3–0.5 of its height, lower when open-grown [est].
- **Crown lobing.** The treetop is "many different sized and shaped spheres" [src](https://pixeljoint.com/forum/printer_friendly_posts.asp?TID=11051). Leaf clusters "follow where the branches get smaller toward the outside of the tree's crown", and they may overlap because some branches come toward the viewer [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7610). Weber and Penn give lobes as odd counts (3, 5, 7) "to avoid obvious symmetry" [src](https://courses.cs.duke.edu/fall02/cps124/resources/p119-weber.pdf).
- **Envelopes.** Weber and Penn's crown shapes are conical, spherical, hemispherical, cylindrical, tapered cylindrical, flame, inverse conical and tend flame. Their example trees: quaking aspen tend flame, black tupelo tapered cylinder, weeping willow cylinder, black oak hemisphere [src](https://courses.cs.duke.edu/fall02/cps124/resources/p119-weber.pdf).
- **Sky holes and branches.** "The branches will always show through a little" [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7610). Sky holes go in the gaps between clusters, close to the branches, painted "darker, duller and cooler" than the sky [src](https://swintonsart.com/post/painting-secret-44-sky-holes-in-trees).
- **Variation within one species** comes from the same rules played out differently. In the space-colonization model, a crown envelope filled with attraction points sets the shape. Fewer points (12,000 down to 375 in the paper) give sparser, more irregular crowns, narrow envelopes give a clear trunk, and wide ones give heavy limbs [src](https://www.algorithmicbotany.org/papers/colonization.egwnp2007.pdf).

## 3. How stylized games and illustrators draw trees with variety and without looking cartoony

- **Firewatch.** "Flat-shapes with strong, distinctive silhouettes with abstract internal details", in distinct colour layers with fog for depth [src](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch). Jane Ng hand-modelled 23 trees [src](https://80.lv/articles/creating-the-world-of-firewatch), and one analysis counts 14 tree models filling a forest of 4,600 trees [src](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/05/how-to-make-stylized-witness-trees-in.html). Few designs, varied per instance.
- **Ori.** Over 90 unique tree graphics, plus leaf and branch graphics combined, each with its own lighting, colour and movement [src](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/?p=4339). Will of the Wisps hand-painted a light-direction map for every asset to light 2D art [src](https://www.thegamer.com/ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-art-interview/).
- **Sable.** Flat shading, with depth from lighting, fog (which "solved a bunch of problems") and outlines that fade with distance [src](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability). **Alto's Odyssey:** "how far can you simplify a form until you're left only with its essence" [src](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-team-alto-s-i-alto-s-odyssey-i-), with a palette "pared down to a handful of hues" [src](https://design-milk.com/altos-adventure-keeps-simple/).
- **Ghibli (Kazuo Oga).** "Until then, I'd painted trees rather symbolically"; for Totoro, "sawtooth oaks, konara oaks and camphors" [src](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/defining-the-world-of-my-neighbor). He "extracts the essence of important elements" but still renders the little things [src](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/what-kazuo-oga-thinks-about-when).
- **Eyvind Earle and Sleeping Beauty.** "Forests built from geometry", "gothic verticals" with "medieval tapestry flatness" [src](https://cuckoocomics.com/eyvind_earle.html). Block "large, dark shapes" along horizontals and verticals, build from dark greys up to small bright highlights, and use broken edges and speckle [src](https://zoungyart.substack.com/p/painting-in-the-style-of-sleeping). Light defines where detail is: ornate bark where a streak of light crosses, less detail in shadow [src](https://d23.com/sleeping-beauty-stunning-art/).
- **An Earle-style tree, procedurally** (SIGGRAPH 2014 poster). The canopy is "three blobbies that are slightly jittered in size and position per tree instance", plus flattened sub-canopies, and "the closer a tree is, the more sub-canopies it has". Foliage marks are biased to the rim and toward the light, and shadows are simpler than their trees, "pointy when the casting geometry is curved" [src](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2014-Poster-97-Murphy_Stylized-Trees-and-Landscapes.pdf).
- **Charley Harper.** The limits of silk screen pushed him "to express my ideas in ever simpler terms" [src](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/articles/charley-harper-ford-motor-company-and-minimal-realism). Simplify, but keep the species.
- **Stylized 3D crowns.** A crown is a blob with leaves on or inside it, and the leaves' normals point out from the blob, so the crown shades as one volume [src](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/05/how-to-make-stylized-witness-trees-in.html) [src](https://simonschreibt.de/gat/airborn-trees). Cremuss uses sphere-projected normals, an inner gradient as fake occlusion, a top-to-bottom gradient for colour, a random value per leaf, and a fake rim light [src](https://cremuss.artstation.com/projects/Vyz12R).
- **Weber and Penn.** Every parameter has a V twin for random variation (nDownAngleV, nCurveV, ScaleV, nLengthV), and one file per species with different seeds gives different individuals. BaseSize is the bare-trunk fraction (0.05 oak and willow to 0.4 aspen). AttractionUp curls stems up or down (0.8 oak, −3 weeping willow). A pruning envelope bounds the crown [src](https://courses.cs.duke.edu/fall02/cps124/resources/p119-weber.pdf).
- **SpeedTree.** Randomizing re-rolls seeds within each property's variance, and "small variances in properties like Length and Start Angle" change a tree without losing its identity [src](https://docs.unity3d.com/speedtree-modeler/manual/randomization.html). Gravity pulls branches down or pushes them up, and noise makes them wander [src](https://docs.unity3d.com/speedtree-modeler/manual/branch-generator-properties.html). Its LOD removes small branches first and enlarges the surviving leaves "to help maintain the same leaf cloud volume" [src](https://docs.speedtree.com/doku.php?id=lod).
- **Engines.** Unity randomizes tree height and width each from 0.01 to 2 times the original, unlocks width from height, and adds colour variation and random rotation [src](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/terrain-Trees.html). It picks each tree's hue-variation strength from its world position [src](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.shadergraph@12.0/manual/SpeedTree8-SubGraphAssets.html), with a variation colour and an amount in alpha [src](https://docs.unity3d.com/2023.1/Documentation/ScriptReference/SpeedTreeImporter-hueVariation.html). Unreal tilts each instance by a Random Pitch Angle "up to the specified angle in degrees", and scales instances by age along a Scale Curve [src](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/open-world-tools-property-reference-in-unreal-engine).
- **L-systems.** Honda's monopodial tree uses contraction ratios r1 0.9 and r2 0.6, branch angles of 45° and a divergence of 137.5°; tropism bends branches with a susceptibility e of 0.14–0.40 [src](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/abop/abop-ch2.pdf).

## 4. Shading trees as volumes

- **One volume first.** Shade the crown as one blob, then break it into clumps. The stylized-tree makers transfer the blob's normals to every leaf so the shading is one soft gradient across the crown [src](https://simonschreibt.de/gat/airborn-trees) [src](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/05/how-to-make-stylized-witness-trees-in.html).
- **Light side and core.** Start dark, add light, and keep "the line that divides light from shadow" uneven [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/zh-tw/articles/7585?org=1). Leonardo: dense trees (laurel, box, holm oak) "have their darkest shadows nearest to the middle of the tree" [src](https://interconnected.org/more/davinci/436.html); the part "farthest from the earth" lies "in the least dark shadow" [src](https://interconnected.org/more/davinci/436.html).
- **Occlusion toward the trunk and the bottom.** Cremuss darkens the inner shell "to give it depth and add a fake occlusion effect" [src](https://cremuss.artstation.com/projects/Vyz12R). Crysis lights vegetation with a hemisphere ambient, lerp(0.5·ambient, ambient, N.z·0.5 + 0.5), so downward faces get half the ambient, and multiplies by baked occlusion [src](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/GPUGems3/gpugems3_ch16.html). Paint darker trunk and branches "where the leaf cover is thicker" [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7610).
- **Rim.** The Earle-style trees bias their foliage marks "the edges (rim light) and in the direction of incoming light" [src](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2014-Poster-97-Murphy_Stylized-Trees-and-Landscapes.pdf). Cremuss adds a fake rim light last [src](https://cremuss.artstation.com/projects/Vyz12R).
- **Cast shadow.** Stylized shadows are "much simpler than the object casting the shadow" [src](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2014-Poster-97-Murphy_Stylized-Trees-and-Landscapes.pdf). Draw a trunk band plus one crown blob laid away from the sun, darkest at the trunk's foot [est].
- **Framing darks.** "It is very effective to show some branches which are low down and dark" to set off lit greens farther off [src](https://interconnected.org/more/davinci/428.html).
- **Connectedness: no floating clumps.** Clumps sit at branch ends [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7610), and needles at the ends of branches [src](https://www.unisoncolour.com/blog/combating-cookie-cutter-conifers-by-tracey-maras/). Sky holes go between clusters but close to the branches [src](https://swintonsart.com/post/painting-secret-44-sky-holes-in-trees). Rule: build the limb skeleton first and hang every clump on a limb end. Every clump either contains a point of its limb or overlaps a clump that does, by at least 20% of the smaller radius. Check the clump graph connects to the trunk before painting, and drop or pull in any island [est].

## 5. Close up: texture, translucency and subsurface scattering

- **Detail grows as the tree nears.** Closer trees have more sub-canopies [src](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2014-Poster-97-Murphy_Stylized-Trees-and-Landscapes.pdf), and distant ones need little detail [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/zh-tw/articles/7585?org=1). In close-ups you see "the individual branches and stems between the clusters of leaves" [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7610).
- **Texture lives in the halftone.** A trunk's directly lit area has "the brightest hue and the least texture" [src](https://tips.clip-studio.com/zh-tw/articles/7585?org=1), and shadow has less detail [src](https://d23.com/sleeping-beauty-stunning-art/). So put leaf and bark marks in the band between full light and shadow, faint in full light and absent in deep shade [est].
- **Why leaves glow.** Chlorophyll absorbs least near 550 nm, and little absorbs from 750 to 1250 nm; chlorosis raises visible transmission [src](https://sites.bu.edu/cliveg/files/2023/10/leaf-optics-jacquemoud-ustin.pdf). A leaf absorbs 50–90% of green light against 80–95% of blue and red [src](https://cheatsheets.davidveksler.com/why-is-grass-green.html), and natural leaves transmit little visible light overall [src](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10033640). So the glow is weak and green-shifted, and stronger in pale young, autumn and chlorotic leaves [est].
- **What painters see.** Seen from the far side from the light, a leaf is "transparent, with a beautiful green colour verging on yellow" [src](https://sacred-texts.com/aor/dv/0431.htm). The transparent lights "show more strongly" where one green has another behind it than against the bright sky [src](https://interconnected.org/more/davinci/428.html). Among leaves between the light and the eye, the nearest are darkest [src](https://interconnected.org/more/davinci/433.html). So the glow belongs on clumps backed by darker foliage, and the silhouette against the sky stays dark [est].
- **Crysis's leaf term.** The back term is (E·−L)⁴, blended by an artist weight with a wrapped saturate(−N·L·0.6 + 0.4), then multiplied by a painted subsurface map (thickness and veins, 128×128 is enough) and by shadow [src](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/GPUGems3/gpugems3_ch16.html). The fourth power keeps the glow within about 40° of directly behind (0.34 at 40°, 0.06 at 60°) [est].
- **Wrap lighting.** Diffuse = max(0, (N·L + w)/(1 + w)), with w from 0 to 1. Scattering shifts the colour at the terminator; the skin example uses a band of width 0.3 in red (0.15, 0, 0) [src](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-iii-materials/chapter-16-real-time-approximations-subsurface-scattering). For leaves use yellow-green in that band [est].
- **Frostbite's translucency.** I = saturate(V·−(L + N·δ))^p · s: δ pulls the back light toward the normal, p sets the falloff and s the strength [src](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2017/08/30/fast-subsurface-scattering-1/).
- **Engines.** Unreal's Two Sided Foliage lets light through so undersides are not near-black; its Subsurface Color sets "the amount of light transmission" and masks stems and veins [src](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/shading-models-in-unreal-engine). HDRP has a Transmission Tint, a Thickness Remap, and a Thin Object mode for double-sided leaves [src](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/hdrp/manual/Diffusion-Profile.html). Without some translucency, unlit leaf faces go very dark [src](https://simonschreibt.de/gat/airborn-trees).

## 6. What we take: buildable rules for the canvas-2D tree painter

**6.1 Per-tree variation.** Draw every value in §3's table from its own keyed stream (pkSub index per attribute), so a tree keeps its shape when detail tiers switch on [est]. Group them as size (height, width), pose (lean, offset toward the open side or the light, flag side), structure (age, bare trunk, tiers, per-tier length and angle jitter, missing branches, stubs, leader), and colour (hue ±4°, value ±6%, saturation ±8%), all applied before drawing [est]. Edge and slope trees lean and offset toward the open or downhill side; exposed ridge trees flag away from the world's wind [est].

**6.2 Conifer construction that reads as layered branches** [est throughout, from §1].
1. Envelope: the half-width at height u is W/2·f(u), with f from Weber and Penn's shapes: conical for spruce, fir, young pine and larch; tapered cylinder for old spruce; flame or cylinder for cypress; a flat cap for old pine and cedar.
2. Trunk to the very tip, tapering, leaning by the instance's lean; spruce and fir trunks are 0.03–0.05 h wide at the base.
3. Tiers: N = clamp(round(liveCrownPx / 6 px), 4, 14), positions jittered ±15% of the spacing, at least 3 px apart at canonical size.
4. Each tier is a left and a right branch pad, plus a front pad crossing the trunk on 1 tier in 3. A pad is a spine from the trunk at the species angle and length f(u)·(1 ± 0.15), with an irregular 6–9 point polygon around it. Its top edge sits 0.15–0.3 of the spacing above the spine. Its lower edge droops 0.6–0.9 of the spacing for spruce, broken into 3–6 hanging tufts; 0.3–0.4 for fir, straight and dense; for pine, tufts only on the outer 40% of each limb, with the bare limb showing inward.
5. Angles: spruce −15° to −30° with the tip lifted 10–20°; fir −5° to +5°, stiff; young pine +15° to +35°; cedar and larch level, cedar with long flat plates and wide sky between shelves, larch with a hanging fringe and 30–40% see-through.
6. Drop 10–25% of pads and shorten 1 tier in 6 by 30%, so the outline is a row of uneven tips and the trunk shows in a few gaps.
7. Paint order: back pads in deep, trunk, front pads in dark. Then each pad's top 40% in body, a lit strip on the sun-side pad tops, and lit needle tips. The core stays darkest.
8. Top: a leader spike with 2–3 tiny tiers; double leaders and broken tops per §3. Below the live crown, dead stubs on forest trees.
9. Exposure: flag (windward pads cut to 0–30% length above the abrasion band, which is about 1 m above the snow line); krummholz (a mat 1–2 m tall, wider than tall, combed leeward); coast cypress (flat leeward layers).

**6.3 Broadleaf crowns and connectedness** [est]. Limb skeleton first: trunk, 2–4 forks at 0.3–0.5 h (lower when open-grown), each limb ending at a lobe centre. Use 3, 5 or 7 main lobes of 5–9 clumps each, inside the species envelope (oak hemisphere W:H 0.9–1.1 open-grown, beech dome, aspen tend flame, poplar flame), with the crown offset toward the open side. Apply the connectedness rule of §4 to every kind, the pine, stone pine, poplar and birch first. Draw 0–3 sky holes in DDC colour between lobes near a limb, only on crowns over 30 px, and let limbs show through 1–3 gaps.

**6.4 Shading layers, in order, for every tree** (palms, bamboo, ferns, cacti and dead trees included) [est].
1. Cast shadow: pkFoot's ellipse plus a crown blob laid away from the sun, darkest at the trunk's contact.
2. Back mass in deep (dark 25% toward black).
3. Trunk and limbs: a lit strip on the sun side, darkened about 20% under the crown.
4. Crown in dark.
5. Sky-down ramp over the crown: +8% value at the top to −10% at the bottom, in 2–3 flat bands, not a smooth gradient.
6. Body clumps offset 0.2 r toward the light; the lit set widened by wrap (lt > −0.4).
7. Core occlusion: one dark ellipse at alpha 0.25 low in the crown's centre, toward the trunk.
8. Lit clumps, small, only on the sun-side top.
9. Rim: front-lit, a cool sky rim on the top edge at alpha 0.3; backlit, a 1–2 px edge in mix(sky, sun, 0.5) on the sun side.
10. Sky holes.
11. Near layers (6.5, 6.6).

**6.5 Near-camera texture** (canonical px of the 3072 export) [est].
- Crown radius under 10 px: the far discs as now. 10–40 px: ragged clumps as now.
- Over 40 px: 6–14 leaf marks per lit or halftone clump in the species' leaf shape (beech oval, oak lobed, birch small and toothed), with needle strokes fanning from conifer pad spines.
- Over 80 px: separate leaves on the silhouette, twigs between clusters, veins on glowing leaves.
- Trunk over 5 px: grooves as now. Over 12 px: the species pattern from §5's table (pine plates with dark seams, oak ridges, beech smooth with horizontal etchings and bosses, birch white with dark diamonds and a black foot, cherry lenticel bands), drawn only in the lit half and the halftone.

**6.6 Translucency and a faked subsurface scattering, near only** (crown radius over 25 px) [est except where tagged].
1. Backlight: k = max(0, −cos θ), θ being the angle between the sun's azimuth and the camera's view; B = k⁴, the Crysis exponent [src](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/GPUGems3/gpugems3_ch16.html), times a low-sun boost clamp(1.3 − elevation/45°, 0.3, 1).
2. Thickness per clump: 0 in the outer 30% of the crown's radius, rising to 1 at the core and on clumps with sky behind them. Leonardo puts the glow where green is behind green [src](https://interconnected.org/more/davinci/428.html).
3. Glow colour: mix(lit, rgb(196, 222, 84), 0.5), then 30% toward the sun's colour. That is "green verging on yellow" [src](https://sacred-texts.com/aor/dv/0431.htm).
4. Composite: screen at alpha s·B·(1 − thickness). Strength s: thin beech, birch and maple 0.6; oak 0.35; young spring and autumn leaves 0.8 (chlorosis raises transmission [src](https://sites.bu.edu/cliveg/files/2023/10/leaf-optics-jacquemoud-ustin.pdf)); conifer pads 0.15, on their fringes only.
5. Veins over 80 px: 1 px strokes in dark at 40% opacity on glowing leaves, as Unreal's subsurface mask does for stems and veins [src](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/shading-models-in-unreal-engine).
6. Front-lit SSS: a terminator band between dark and body clumps, about 0.3 r wide (Green's 0.3 [src](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-iii-materials/chapter-16-real-time-approximations-subsurface-scattering)), in a saturated yellow-green at alpha 0.2.
7. Keep the silhouette against the sky dark: the nearest leaves between light and eye are darkest [src](https://interconnected.org/more/davinci/433.html).
